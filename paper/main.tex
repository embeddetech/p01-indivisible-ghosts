\begin{abstract}
Barandes' stochastic--quantum correspondence represents unitary quantum
theory by \emph{indivisible} stochastic processes on a configuration space,
while the antilinearity program of Bender and Mannheim replaces Hermiticity
by $\PT$ symmetry, recovering unitarity in a modified inner product. We ask
whether these two reconstructions are compatible, and follow the question
from the canonical $2\times 2$ $\PT$ dimer to interacting
Pais--Uhlenbeck-type ghosts. Our results: (i)~unbroken antilinear symmetry
is exactly the condition for a probability-conserving process
to exist \emph{without enlarging the configuration space} (via a
time-independent beable map)---on
beables generically rotated from the original
configurations---and the metric-corrected process is the ordinary
(generically indivisible) process of the
isospectral Hermitian partner; (ii)~a non-Hermitian Hamiltonian admits a
\emph{fixed-beable} representation iff its on-site energies are real and
its couplings are two-way with positive pair products obeying a
Kolmogorov cycle (detailed-balance) condition, and the resulting process is unique---in gauge
language, fixed beables exist precisely when the imaginary gauge flux
vanishes---the exact obstruction, of which the non-Hermitian skin effect
is the spectral manifestation;
(iii)~the broken phase, treated as an open subsystem by dilation, supports a
valid sub-stochastic process whose indivisibility is \emph{terminal} rather
than recurrent---we prove exact closed forms, including the generator floor
$-(\sin\theta+\tilde\kappa)$ (a floor on the indivisibility strength
$|L_{12}|$, approached from below by the signed entry)---while
late-time information backflow vanishes numerically: the two
notions of non-Markovianity dissociate; (iv)~the exceptional point is a
coordinate singularity of the Hermitian dictionary, not of the process;
(v)~the free Pais--Uhlenbeck ghost admits a perfectly valid fixed-beable
process on the truncated configuration space, whereas the no-ghost
quantization exits the sample space;
(vi)~interacting ghosts realize the probabilist's classification
conservative/transient/explosive (the last at the mechanism level), and (vii)~a deficiency-index/Feller
analysis proves an ``explosion dictionary'' for the one-dimensional
monotone mechanism model: one classical integral decides
classical completeness, quantum self-adjointness (its limit-circle leg under $C^{2}$ error control), and stochastic
conservativity, with (viii)~a rigorous channel-based multidimensional
extension (for separable, central, and boundedly-coupled model classes) in which conservative completions carry an infinite-dimensional,
channel-mixing $S$-matrix at infinity. For the exact quartic
Pais--Uhlenbeck operator we further prove an exact displaced-oscillator
identity whose channelization (exact up to $O(k^{2})$) carries a sign
prediction the numerics confirm ($\lambda>0$: limit-circle channels;
$\lambda<0$: a quantum-completion candidate despite classical blowup),
settle deficiency stability at every finite Galerkin channel number, certify the
one-dimensional fiber non-self-adjoint, in a computer-assisted sense,
by a glued-tail von Neumann
certificate---no compactly supported function can witness
deficiency---and conjecturally reduce the remaining $M\to\infty$
step to Gaussian-beam
assembly and error control along numerically characterized escape
rays. The ghost problem,
viewed in the
stochastic currency, is a recurrence-classification problem, and the
minimal explosive process is its only canonical resolution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Two contemporary research programs demote the standard Hilbert-space
formalism of quantum mechanics to a derived structure, and both elevate the
role of time reversal.

The first is Barandes' \emph{stochastic--quantum correspondence}
\cite{Barandes2023,Barandes2025}: quantum theory is equivalent to a class of
generalized stochastic processes on a configuration space, in which a
definite configuration (a ``beable'') exists at all times and the transition
matrix $\Gam(t)$ fails to factorize between measurements. Such processes are
\emph{indivisible}: except at special times, no stochastic matrix
$\Gam(t_2,t_1)$ satisfies $\Gam(t_2)=\Gam(t_2,t_1)\Gam(t_1)$. Unitarity, in
this reading, is not an axiom but a representation-theoretic consequence.

The second is the antilinearity program of Bender, Mannheim, and
collaborators \cite{BenderBoettcher1998,Bender2007,Mannheim2018}: the
correct organizing principle of quantum theory is not Hermiticity but
invariance under an antilinear symmetry such as $\PT$. A $\PT$-symmetric,
non-Hermitian Hamiltonian with real spectrum and a complete set of
symmetry-respecting eigenvectors (``unbroken phase'') evolves
unitarily with respect to a modified inner product; when eigenvalues form
complex-conjugate pairs (``broken phase''), exponentially growing and
decaying modes balance in a biorthogonal pairing. The program's flagship
application is the fourth-order Pais--Uhlenbeck (PU) oscillator
\cite{PaisUhlenbeck1950}, the quantum-mechanical prototype of conformal
(Weyl) gravity \cite{Mannheim2012}, whose Ostrogradski ghost is claimed to
be exorcised by $\PT$-symmetric quantization \cite{BenderMannheim2008}.

These programs have, to our knowledge, never been brought into contact
(a recent study of divisibility within the correspondence
\cite{DivisibleIndivisible2025} remains within the standard
Hermitian/unitary-generated setting and does not treat non-Hermitian
or antilinear dynamics).
Barandes' construction rests on ordinary unitarity in a fixed inner product;
Mannheim's unitarity lives in a Hamiltonian-dependent inner product. The
natural question is:

\begin{quote}
\emph{Can antilinear ($\PT$) symmetry predict or produce indivisible
stochastic processes---and what becomes of the correspondence in the broken
phase, at exceptional points, and for the ghosts it was designed to save?}
\end{quote}

This paper answers the question in stages, each stage exactly solvable or
numerically verified, and each feeding the next. Our main results:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Existence gates, but does not generate}
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:deflation}). Unbroken antilinear symmetry is precisely the
  condition for a valid probability-conserving, same-configuration-space
  process to exist (via a time-independent beable map; generically indivisible); however, the
  metric-corrected process is the \emph{ordinary} Barandes process of the
  isospectral Hermitian partner $h=\rho H\rho^{-1}$
  \cite{Mostafazadeh2002}. Closed-system $\PT$ machinery is an isomorphism,
  not a generalization. Its two costs are dynamics-dependent beables and
  metric non-uniqueness.
\item \textbf{Fixed beables $\Leftrightarrow$ detailed balance}
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:kolmogorov}). The \emph{detailed-balance theorem}
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:kolmogorov}): a
  non-Hermitian $H$ admits a beable-preserving (diagonal-metric)
  representation iff its on-site energies are real and its couplings
  are two-way with positive pair products satisfying Kolmogorov-type detailed-balance
  conditions; the metric weights are the inverse stationary distribution,
  the spectrum is automatically real, and the fixed-beable process is
  unique---both costs dissolve exactly on this class. In gauge variables
  the criterion reads: the \emph{imaginary} gauge flux must vanish while
  real magnetic flux is unconstrained, so the imaginary gauge flux is
  precisely the obstruction to fixed beables---finer than net spectral
  winding (Corollary~\ref{cor:topology})---of which the non-Hermitian skin
  effect \cite{HatanoNelson1996,Okuma2020} is the spectral manifestation
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:skin}).
\item \textbf{The broken phase is a valid open process; the two
  non-Markovianities dissociate} (Sec.~\ref{sec:broken}). Broken-$\PT$
  evolution is survival-conditioned dynamics of a contraction; its unitary
  dilation defines an ordinary indivisible process whose reduction is
  sub-stochastic. The \emph{terminal-indivisibility theorem}
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:terminal}) gives the generator in closed
  form: divisibility is lost at $t_c$ exactly when $\det\Gam$ crosses zero,
  permanently, with floor $-(\sin\theta+\tilde\kappa)$; the unbroken phase
  is recurrent instead. Late-window information backflow, by contrast,
  vanishes (numerically) in the
  broken phase: Barandes indivisibility is the strictly finer probe.
\item \textbf{The exceptional point is a coordinate singularity}
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:ep}). The metric collapses with Petermann scaling
  $\mathrm{cond}(\eta)\sim\varepsilon^{-1}$ and the lab-time
  metric-corrected process freezes to the identity; a nontrivial critical
  process survives only on the rescaled clock $\tau=\kappa t$, in
  coordinates whose beable map diverges. The open-system description passes
  through the exceptional point smoothly.
\item \textbf{Pais--Uhlenbeck: the two programs pay in different
  currencies} (Sec.~\ref{sec:pu}). The Hermitian Ostrogradski realization
  of free PU is a valid, fixed-beable, indivisible process on a truncated
  (discretized) configuration space of physical
  $(x,\dot x)$---the unbounded-below energy spectrum is
  invisible to the process. The $\PT$/no-ghost quantization is a domain
  change whose vacuum has no shadow at any truncation: not a rotation of
  beables but an exit from the sample space.
\item \textbf{Interacting ghosts: conservative, transient, explosive}
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:interacting}). Quadratic interactions collapse the
  instability threshold from the sum to the difference frequency and make
  the process \emph{transient} (exact cascade laws), yet it remains a valid
  doubly stochastic process with balanced energy books. Malicious nonlinear
  interactions with finite-time classical escape make it
  \emph{explosive}---at the level of the classical mechanism (its minimal
  escape process, Sec.~\ref{sec:dictionary}); the quantum operator can
  resist (the $\lambda<0$ completion candidates of
  Sec.~\ref{sec:multiD}).
\item \textbf{The explosion dictionary} (Sec.~\ref{sec:dictionary}).
  The \emph{explosion dictionary} (Theorem~\ref{thm:dictionary}, for
  the one-dimensional monotone mechanism model; Sec.~\ref{sec:multiD}
  records where it fails beyond that model): a
  single classical integral---the time of
  flight to infinity---decides classical completeness, quantum essential
  self-adjointness (via the WKB identity ``deficiency norm $\approx$
  time of
  flight'', its limit-circle leg under $C^{2}$ error control), and stochastic conservativity (Feller). Conservative
  completions reflect probability off infinity at the classical round-trip
  time; the canonical object is the minimal, explosive, sub-stochastic
  process.
\item \textbf{Multidimensional channel theory}
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:multiD}). Since classical incompleteness and quantum
  non-self-adjointness can come apart \cite{RauchReed1973}, we
  prove channel-based statements: quadratic ghosts are rigorously safe
  (Nelson's theorem); separable, central, and boundedly-coupled malicious
  systems have deficiency indices $(\infty,\infty)$, so conservative
  completions carry a channel-mixing \emph{$S$-matrix at infinity}. The
  essential self-adjointness of the exact quartic-PU operator is posed as
  a sharply delimited open problem---and then taken as far as computation
  allows: an exact displaced-oscillator identity, its channelization
  (exact up to $O(k^{2})$) carrying a confirmed sign prediction
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:bo}), deficiency stability at every finite
  Galerkin channel number (Theorem~\ref{thm:stability}) with its $\lambda<0$
  mirror (Corollary~\ref{cor:dichotomy}), the no-compact-witness
  principle (Remark~\ref{rem:nocompact}) together with a computer-assisted
  glued-tail von Neumann certificate for the one-dimensional fiber, and a
  Maslov feasibility study conjecturally reducing the remaining
  $M\to\infty$ step to
  Gaussian-beam assembly and error control along the escape rays.
\end{enumerate}

\part{The correspondence machinery}

\section{Preliminaries and the deflationary observation}
\label{sec:deflation}

\subsection{The stochastic--quantum correspondence}

Following \cite{Barandes2023,Barandes2025}, a \emph{generalized stochastic
system} on a configuration set $\{1,\dots,n\}$ is specified by a family of
column-stochastic matrices $\Gam(t)$, $\Gam_{ij}(t)\ge 0$,
$\sum_i\Gam_{ij}(t)=1$, giving the probability of configuration $i$ at time
$t$ given $j$ at time $0$. The system is \emph{divisible} on $(t_1,t_2)$ if
there is a stochastic matrix $\Gam(t_2,t_1)$ with
$\Gam(t_2)=\Gam(t_2,t_1)\,\Gam(t_1)$, and \emph{indivisible} if such
factorizations fail except at isolated times (``division events'').
The stochastic--quantum dictionary represents
\begin{equation}
\Gam(t)=\Theta(t)\odot\Theta^{*}(t),
\qquad
\Theta(t)=U(t)=\ee^{-\ii H t},
\qquad
\Gam_{ij}=|U_{ij}|^{2},
\label{eq:dictionary}
\end{equation}
with $\odot$ the entrywise product; $|U|^{2}$ is doubly stochastic if $U$
is unitary (the converse fails: stochasticity of $|U|^2$ fixes only
the moduli), and indivisibility expresses interference,
$|UV|^{2}\ne|U|^{2}|V|^{2}$. Two facts matter below: the family
$\Gam(t)$ need not compose---a \emph{necessary} signature of
indivisibility, whose operative test is the negativity of the divisor
\eqref{eq:divisor}, not the mere size of $\Gam(2t)-\Gam(t)^{2}$---and
only per-time unistochasticity is required.

If $\Gam(t_1)$ is invertible, the candidate divisor is unique,
\begin{equation}
M=\Gam(t_2)\,\Gam(t_1)^{-1},
\label{eq:divisor}
\end{equation}
and has unit column sums automatically; divisibility on $(t_1,t_2)$ holds
iff $M$ is entrywise nonnegative (with column sums $\le 1$ in the
sub-stochastic case). Infinitesimally, with the generator
$L(t)=\dot\Gam(t)\Gam(t)^{-1}$, divisibility across $t$ (classical
P-divisibility) holds iff the off-diagonal entries of $L(t)$ are
nonnegative (with column sums of $L(t)$ nonpositive in the
sub-stochastic case).

\subsection{Pseudo-Hermitian and \texorpdfstring{$\PT$}{PT}-symmetric quantum mechanics}

A diagonalizable $H$ with antilinear symmetry has eigenvalues either real
or in complex-conjugate pairs \cite{Bender2007,Mannheim2018}. If the
spectrum is real and a positive-definite metric $\eta$ exists with
\begin{equation}
H^{\dagger}\eta=\eta H,
\label{eq:intertwine}
\end{equation}
then $\rho=\eta^{1/2}$ furnishes a Hermitian partner
$h=\rho H\rho^{-1}$ and a unitary $u(t)=\rho\,\ee^{-\ii Ht}\rho^{-1}$
\cite{Mostafazadeh2002}. The canonical biorthogonal choice is
$\eta=(VV^{\dagger})^{-1}$, $V$ the right-eigenvector matrix. Our
laboratory is the canonical dimer
\begin{equation}
H(\theta,s)=
\begin{pmatrix}
\ee^{\ii\theta} & s\\[2pt]
s & \ee^{-\ii\theta}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
E_{\pm}=\cos\theta\pm\sqrt{s^{2}-\sin^{2}\theta},
\label{eq:dimer}
\end{equation}
which is $\PT$-symmetric ($PH^{*}P=H$, $P=\sigma_x$): unbroken for
$s>\sin\theta$, broken for $s<\sin\theta$, with an exceptional point (EP)
at $s_{*}=\sin\theta$.

\subsection{Existence gates; deflation; two costs}

Throughout we use $\theta=\pi/4$ and, unless stated, $t=1.3$.

\begin{proposition}[metric-corrected process; deflation]
\label{prop:deflation}
\emergencystretch=1.5em
In the unbroken phase, $\Gam(t)=|\rho\,\ee^{-\ii Ht}\rho^{-1}|^{2}$ is
doubly stochastic and, at generic tested parameters, indivisible; in the broken phase no
positive-definite solution of \eqref{eq:intertwine} exists, and---for any
fixed invertible beable map---no probability-conserving process of this
form exists (time-dependent identifications, as on the rescaled clock of
Sec.~\ref{sec:ep}, are a separate question). Moreover $\rho\,\ee^{-\ii Ht}\rho^{-1}=\ee^{-\ii ht}$: the
metric-corrected process is the ordinary Barandes process of the Hermitian
partner $h$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $\eta=\rho^{2}$ solves \eqref{eq:intertwine} with $\rho$ positive
definite, then $h:=\rho H\rho^{-1}$ obeys
$h^{\dagger}=\rho^{-1}H^{\dagger}\rho
=\rho^{-1}(\eta H\eta^{-1})\rho=h$, so $h$ is Hermitian,
$\rho\,\ee^{-\ii Ht}\rho^{-1}=\ee^{-\ii ht}$ is unitary, and the
entrywise moduli-squared of a unitary are doubly stochastic
(unistochastic). Indivisibility at generic $t$ is exhibited
numerically below. In the broken phase the spectrum contains a
complex-conjugate pair; similarity to a Hermitian $h$ would force a
real spectrum, so no positive-definite solution of
\eqref{eq:intertwine}---and hence no process of this form---exists. The
exclusion is in fact similarity-free: for any fixed invertible $B$,
column-stochasticity of $|B\ee^{-\ii Ht}B^{-1}|^{2}$ makes
$\norm{B\ee^{-\ii Ht}B^{-1}}=\sqrt n$ (Frobenius) bounded in $t$, yet the
Frobenius norm dominates the spectral radius, which is
similarity-invariant:
$\sqrt n\ge\operatorname{spr}(B\ee^{-\ii Ht}B^{-1})=\operatorname{spr}(\ee^{-\ii Ht})=\ee^{\beta t}$,
$\beta=\max_k\mathrm{Im}\,E_k>0$ in the broken phase---impossible for
large $t$. Hence no invertible $B$ yields a probability-conserving
process on any beables. The same constraint closes the boundary case
$s=s_*$: column-stochasticity forces $\norm{B\ee^{-\ii Ht}B^{-1}}=\sqrt n$
at \emph{every} $t$, so $\{\ee^{-\ii Ht}\}_{t\ge0}$ is similar, via the fixed
$B$, to a bounded family---impossible for a defective $H$, whose Jordan
block makes $\norm{\ee^{-\ii Ht}}$ grow linearly in $t$. Real
\emph{diagonalizable} spectrum is thus necessary, covering the exceptional
point as well.
\end{proof}

Numerically, at $s=1$ the naive column sums of $|U|^2$ are $(3.23,1.30)$
(here and throughout, $\norm{\cdot}$ on matrices is the Frobenius
norm), while the corrected process is
$\Gam=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0.368&0.632\\0.632&0.368\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$,
indivisible at this $t$: the divisor \eqref{eq:divisor} has a negative
entry, $\min M=-1.13$ (the family also fails to compose,
$\norm{\Gam(2t)-\Gam(t)^{2}}\approx 0.93$); at $s=0.5$ (spectrum
$0.7071\pm 0.5\ii$) every Hermitian intertwiner is indefinite and
$\norm{\ee^{-\ii Ht}}$ grows as $1.58,\,2.04,\,3.61,\,10.4,\,77.2$ at
$t=0.5,1,2,4,8$. Thus unbroken antilinearity is exactly the
\emph{gatekeeper} for process existence, while the process itself is
isomorphic to an ordinary one---closed-system $\PT$ machinery cannot
produce a new class of indivisible processes. The isomorphism carries two
costs: (a)~$\rho$ is generically non-diagonal, so the beables rotate, and
the rotation depends on $H$; (b)~the metric, hence the process, is
non-unique. Both costs are resolved exactly in the next section.

\section{Fixed beables and Kolmogorov detailed balance}
\label{sec:kolmogorov}

We ask which $H$ admit a metric diagonal in the configuration basis, so
that the beables are preserved.

\begin{theorem}[fixed-beable characterization]
\label{thm:kolmogorov}
A matrix $H$ admits a positive diagonal metric $\eta=\mathrm{diag}(d)$,
$d_i>0$, satisfying \eqref{eq:intertwine} iff all of the following hold:
\begin{itemize}
\item[\emph{(K1)}] every diagonal entry $H_{ii}$ is real;
\item[\emph{(K2)}] $H_{ij}=0 \Leftrightarrow H_{ji}=0$;
\item[\emph{(K3)}] $H_{ij}H_{ji}>0$ for every coupled pair $i\ne j$;
\item[\emph{(K4)}] \emph{(Kolmogorov cycle condition)} around every cycle
of the coupling graph, the product of the moduli $|H_{ij}|$ taken one way
equals the product taken the other way.
\end{itemize}
When they hold, $d$ is unique up to one positive scale per connected
component of the coupling graph.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The component form of \eqref{eq:intertwine} with $\eta=\mathrm{diag}(d)$ is
$d_iH_{ij}=d_j\overline{H_{ji}}$. The $i=j$ component gives (K1);
positivity of $d$ forces (K2); the phase of the off-diagonal relation gives
(K3); its modulus gives $d_i/d_j=|H_{ji}|/|H_{ij}|$, whose consistency
around closed loops is (K4). Conversely, define $d$ along a spanning
forest; (K4) makes it well defined, and positivity is automatic.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:kolmogorov}
Under \emph{(K1)--(K4)}:
(i) $h=\eta^{1/2}H\eta^{-1/2}$ is Hermitian, so the spectrum of $H$ is
automatically real---unbroken antilinearity is derived, not assumed;
(ii) with $\rho=\mathrm{diag}(\sqrt d)$ the process is the pure
reweighting
$\Gam_{ij}(t)=(d_i/d_j)\,|U_{ij}(t)|^{2}$,
invariant under the residual scale freedom, hence \emph{unique};
(iii) with classical hopping rates $q(i{\to}j):=|H_{ji}|$, the distribution
$\pi_i\propto 1/d_i$ satisfies detailed balance
$\pi_i q(i{\to}j)=\pi_j q(j{\to}i)$: the metric weights are the inverse
Kolmogorov equilibrium distribution \cite{Kolmogorov1936,Kelly1979};
(iv) the fixed-beable class is exactly the positive-diagonal similarity
orbit of the Hermitian matrices (``detailed-balanced gain/loss'').
\end{corollary}

(A biorthogonal-KMS route to the same metric-versus-detailed-balance
equivalence, for diagonalizable non-Hermitian $H$ of real spectrum, was
given independently by Lan, Ma and Yang~\cite{LanMaYang2026}; the
fixed-beable/Kolmogorov reading, the inverse-stationary weights, and the
skin-effect obstruction below are ours.)

Three instructive cases (all verified numerically, including $200/200$
randomized trials at $n=3,\dots,6$ in which an independent null-space
computation confirms the solution space is exactly the constructive
$d$'s ray):

\emph{(a) $n=2$.} Cycles beyond pairs are absent, so (K4) is vacuous:
fixed-beable $\Leftrightarrow$ $H_{11},H_{22}\in\mathbb R$ and
$H_{12}H_{21}>0$ (or $H_{12}=H_{21}=0$, the decoupled case). The dimer \eqref{eq:dimer} fails (K1) for every
$\theta\ne 0,\pi$: within that family, fixed beables $\Leftrightarrow$
Hermiticity, consistent with the rotation found in
Sec.~\ref{sec:deflation}. The cycle condition first bites at $n\ge 3$.

\emph{(b) A genuinely non-Hermitian fixed-beable $3\times3$ example.}
Take $d=(1,2,4)$, real diagonal $(0.3,-0.4,1.0)$, upper entries
$H_{12}=1+\ii$, $H_{13}=0.8\ii$, $H_{23}=1.5\,\ee^{\ii\pi/5}$ and lower
entries $H_{ji}=(d_i/d_j)\overline{H_{ij}}$. Then
$\norm{H-H^{\dagger}}=1.69$, yet the spectrum is real
$\{-1.319,\,0.210,\,2.009\}$; the naive column sums $(0.40,1.17,3.04)$ are
repaired to a doubly stochastic $\Gam$ by the reweighting alone---no
beable rotation---and the resulting process is indivisible (divisor \eqref{eq:divisor} minimum
entry $-0.26$; the family does not compose, $\norm{\Gam(2t)-\Gam(t)^{2}}\approx0.41$).

\emph{(c) Only (K4) fails.} $H=\mathrm{diag}(1,2,3)$ plus the asymmetric
positive ring with $(H_{12},H_{23},H_{31})=(0.6,0.6,0.6)$ and
$(H_{21},H_{32},H_{13})=(0.3,0.3,0.3)$: cycle products $0.216\ne0.027$.
The spectrum is still real $\{0.847,\,1.840,\,3.313\}$, a positive-definite
(non-diagonal) metric exists and yields a valid indivisible process, but
the diagonal-intertwiner solution space is exactly zero and $\rho$ has an
$18\%$ off-diagonal fraction: \emph{(K4) gates fixed beables specifically,
not process existence.}

\begin{remark}[relation to prior work]
The matrix-algebra core of Theorem~\ref{thm:kolmogorov} is classical:
diagonal symmetrizability of nonnegative matrices was characterized by
Parter and Youngs \cite{ParterYoungs1962}, and cyclic-product criteria for
diagonal similarity by Engel and Schneider \cite{EngelSchneider1973}. The
contribution here is the identification of this class with \emph{beable
preservation} in the stochastic--quantum correspondence, the
detailed-balance dictionary for the metric, the automatic reality of the
spectrum in the pseudo-Hermitian setting, and the uniqueness of the
resulting process. In the reverse direction---non-Hermitian quantum
techniques applied to classical Markov processes violating detailed
balance---see \cite{VanWesemael2025}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Gauge form: the non-Hermitian skin effect is the obstruction}
\label{sec:skin}

Conditions (K2)--(K3) have a gauge-theoretic meaning: they hold iff the
couplings can be written \emph{uniquely} as
\begin{equation}
H_{jk}=t_{jk}\,\ee^{\,\ii\varphi_{jk}+w_{jk}},
\qquad
t_{jk}=t_{kj}>0,\quad
\varphi_{jk}=-\varphi_{kj},\quad
w_{jk}=-w_{kj},
\label{eq:gaugeform}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ as a lattice Peierls substitution $p\to p-A-\ii W$ with a \emph{real}
gauge field $\varphi$ (link variables of $A$) and an \emph{imaginary} gauge
field $w$ (link variables of $W$, the Hatano--Nelson field
\cite{HatanoNelson1996}); (K1) is the absence of on-site gain/loss. In
these variables the detailed-balance theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:kolmogorov}) becomes:

\begin{theorem}[skin obstruction]
\label{thm:skin}
For a gauge-form Hamiltonian \eqref{eq:gaugeform} with real on-site
potential:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] a fixed-beable representation exists iff the \emph{imaginary
flux vanishes through every cycle}, $\sum_{C}w=0$; then $w_{jk}=
\chi_j-\chi_k$ is pure gauge, the metric is the exponentiated imaginary
gauge transformation $\eta=\mathrm{diag}(\ee^{-2\chi})$, and
$\Gam_{jk}(t)=\ee^{2(\chi_k-\chi_j)}|U_{jk}(t)|^{2}$. The \emph{real} flux
(magnetic field) is unconstrained.
\item[(ii)] With classical rates $q(j{\to}k)=|H_{kj}|$, the imaginary flux
is, up to the cycle-orientation convention, half the cycle affinity of the rate
graph \cite{Schnakenberg1976}: it vanishes iff the classical
chain is reversible (Kolmogorov), and nonzero imaginary flux is equivalent
to a nonzero steady-state cycle current \cite{Schnakenberg1976}.
\item[(iii)] (Hatano--Nelson) A ring ($N\ge3$) with uniform $w\ne0$ carries flux $Nw$:
no fixed beables exist. For the \emph{uniform} ring (uniform $t$,
uniform $w$, zero real hopping phase, constant on-site potential) the periodic-boundary
spectrum is moreover a
complex loop (the spectral winding that classifies the skin effect
\cite{Okuma2020})---explicitly $E_k=V_0+2t\cos(k-\ii w)$,
$k\in\tfrac{2\pi}{N}\mathbb{Z}$, non-real for every $N\ge3$ since
$\mathrm{Im}\,E_k=2t\sinh w\,\sin k\ne0$ for $k\ne0,\pi$---so by
Proposition~\ref{prop:deflation} no closed process exists there at all; a
non-uniform ring with nonzero net flux still has no fixed beables,
but its flux can be too
weak to break spectral reality, leaving a closed rotated-beable
process possible (the diagonally dominated triangle below is exactly
such a case). An open chain is a
tree: fixed beables exist at every finite $N$ with weights
$d_i=\ee^{2wi}$; the boundary-condition dichotomy of the skin effect is
the flux/pure-gauge dichotomy.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) restates (K4) in the variables \eqref{eq:gaugeform}: cycle products of
moduli agree in the two orientations iff $\ee^{\sum_C w}=\ee^{-\sum_C w}$.
(ii) is Kolmogorov's criterion applied to $q$, whose cycle ratios are
$\ee^{2\sum_C w}$. (iii) a ring has one independent cycle; a tree has
none.
\end{proof}

Numerically (Fig.~\ref{fig:skin}): over 50 random gauge-form graphs with
random \emph{real} fluxes and pure-gauge $w$, the constructed metric is
$\ee^{-2\chi}$ exactly and the spectrum real; injecting imaginary flux
through any one cycle collapses the diagonal-intertwiner space to zero.
For the Hatano--Nelson chain the OBC skin eigenvectors equal the gauge
factor $\ee^{-wi}$ times the Hermitian-chain eigenvectors to $5\times
10^{-15}$, and $\mathrm{cond}(\eta)=\ee^{2w(N-1)}$ (an analytic identity,
verified to four digits)---so the
fixed-beable representation, while exact at every finite $N$, degenerates
exponentially in the thermodynamic limit, reproducing the unbounded-metric
pathology of Sec.~\ref{sec:pu} in one-dimensional lattice form. On the
classical side, the steady-state ring current vanishes (to $10^{-17}$)
exactly when the imaginary flux does. Finally, in a regime where the flux
is too weak to break spectral reality (a diagonally dominated triangle),
the binary criterion trips at any nonzero flux while the beable rotation
grows continuously with it: the obstruction is quantitative below the
antilinear transition.

\emph{The non-Hermitian skin effect---spectral winding under periodic
boundaries, boundary pileup under open ones---is thus the spectral and
boundary face of the obstruction to a beable-preserving stochastic
representation, whose exact invariant is the imaginary gauge flux (the
$H^{1}$ class of Corollary~\ref{cor:topology}, finer than net winding);
the obstruction equals detailed-balance violation (irreversibility) of
the underlying classical rate graph.}

\begin{corollary}[topological classification of the obstruction]
\label{cor:topology}
For gauge-form couplings on a graph or lattice, the fixed-beable
obstruction is the class of the imaginary gauge field in the first
cohomology $H^{1}(G,\mathbb R)$: the imaginary \emph{field strength} must
vanish through every contractible cycle (no imaginary magnetic flux), and
the imaginary \emph{holonomy} through every non-contractible one (no
winding)---on a $d$-torus, an $\mathbb R^{d}$ of independent windings,
matching the multi-directional classification of skin effects
\cite{Okuma2020}, plus the pointwise plaquette condition that
band-language winding does not see. The obstruction is finer than net
spectral winding: two Hatano--Nelson chains with \emph{opposite} fields
(net winding zero---the doubled structure of the $\mathbb Z_2$ skin
effect \cite{Okuma2020}), coupled by reciprocal rungs, are obstructed
through their inter-chain cycles of circulation $2w$.
\end{corollary}

Numerically (\texttt{scripts/skin\_topology\_beables.py}): on a $3\times4$ torus
the diagonal-metric solution space has dimension $1$ iff both windings
and the plaquette flux vanish, and $0$ if any is switched on; on a
cylinder, ``winding'' along the open direction is gauge-removable while
the periodic direction obstructs---the obstruction lives in $H^1$ of the
actual geometry; and the $\mathbb Z_2$-type ladder has solution-space
dimension $2$ uncoupled (one metric per chain, $\ee^{\pm2wi}$) collapsing
to $0$ at any rung coupling despite zero net winding.

\section{The broken phase as an open subsystem}
\label{sec:broken}

\subsection{Broken \texorpdfstring{$\PT$}{PT} dynamics is survival-conditioned dynamics}

Write \eqref{eq:dimer} as the balanced gain/loss form
$H=\cos\theta\,\mathbf 1+\ii\sin\theta\,\sigma_z+s\,\sigma_x$. Subtracting
the uniform gain,
\begin{equation}
H_{\rm loss}=H-\ii\sin\theta\,\mathbf 1,
\qquad
\ee^{-\ii Ht}=\ee^{\sin\theta\,t}\,C(t),\quad C(t)=\ee^{-\ii H_{\rm loss}t},
\label{eq:lossframe}
\end{equation}
where $C(t)$ is a contraction (singular values $\le 1$; verified to
machine precision). The notorious broken-phase runaway is thus a scalar
normalization: broken-$\PT$ evolution \emph{is} the no-leak-conditioned
dynamics of a lossy system.

For each $t$ the Halmos dilation \cite{Halmos1950,SzNagy2010}
\begin{equation}
U_{\rm big}(t)=
\begin{pmatrix}
C(t) & \sqrt{\mathbf 1-C(t)C(t)^{\dagger}}\\[2pt]
\sqrt{\mathbf 1-C(t)^{\dagger}C(t)} & -C(t)^{\dagger}
\end{pmatrix}
\label{eq:halmos}
\end{equation}
is exactly unitary with $\Gam_{\rm big}(0)=\mathbf 1_4$. Because the
correspondence requires only per-time unistochasticity---the family need
not compose---$\Gam_{\rm big}(t)=|U_{\rm big}(t)|^{2}$ is a Barandes
\emph{indivisible} process (per-time unistochastic, with a negative
divisor entry, not one-parameter-group-generated) on four configurations
$\{1,2,a_1,a_2\}$ (two system sites, two ``leaked'' ancilla
configurations), and the reduction
\begin{equation}
\Gam_{\rm red}(t)=|C(t)|^{2}
\label{eq:gred}
\end{equation}
is a sub-stochastic process (column deficits $=$ leak probabilities), valid
at all times in the phase where the closed construction failed totally.
(A unitary-embedding perspective on $\PT$ dynamics---a ``hidden entangled
partner'' restoring lost information---was developed by Kawabata, Ashida,
and Ueda \cite{KAU2017}; the per-time Halmos dilation used here is its
process-level counterpart. An operator-theoretic dilation of
\emph{indivisible} contraction families to divisible/unitary ones on
graphs was given independently by Dahya~\cite{Dahya2025}, without the
$\PT$/beable or Barandes-process reading developed here.)

\subsection{Exact generator: terminal indivisibility}

\begin{theorem}[terminal indivisibility in the broken phase]
\label{thm:terminal}
Let $0<s<\sin\theta$, $\tilde\kappa=\sqrt{\sin^{2}\theta-s^{2}}>0$, and set (notation local
to this theorem and its proof)
$c=\cosh\tilde\kappa t$, $\sigma=\sinh(\tilde\kappa t)/\tilde\kappa$,
\[
p=c+\sin\theta\,\sigma,\qquad
q=c-\sin\theta\,\sigma,\qquad
r=s\,\sigma .
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
\Gam_{\rm red}(t)=\ee^{-2\sin\theta\,t}
\begin{pmatrix} p^{2} & r^{2}\\ r^{2} & q^{2}\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det\Gam_{\rm red}=\ee^{-4\sin\theta\,t}\,(1-2r^{2}),
\label{eq:gredclosed}
\end{equation}
and the generator $L=\dot\Gam_{\rm red}\Gam_{\rm red}^{-1}$ has the exact
off-diagonal entries
\begin{equation}
L_{12}=\frac{2spr}{1-2r^{2}},
\qquad
L_{21}=\frac{2sqr}{1-2r^{2}} .
\label{eq:generator}
\end{equation}
Consequently:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] the process is P-divisible precisely on $[0,t_c)$ with
$t_c=\tilde\kappa^{-1}\,\mathrm{arcsinh}\!\bigl(\tilde\kappa/(s\sqrt2)\bigr)$,
which is exactly the time at which $\det\Gam_{\rm red}$ crosses zero;
\item[(ii)] $L_{12}<0$ strictly for \emph{all} $t>t_c$: permanent
(terminal) indivisibility;
\item[(iii)] $L_{12}<-(\sin\theta+\tilde\kappa)$ for all $t>t_c$, with
$L_{12}\to-(\sin\theta+\tilde\kappa)$ as $t\to\infty$: the limit is a floor
on $|L_{12}|$, approached from below by the signed entry;
\item[(iv)] $L_{21}$ returns positive at
$t_0=\tilde\kappa^{-1}\,\mathrm{artanh}(\tilde\kappa/\sin\theta)>t_c$:
late-time indivisibility is carried entirely by transitions into the gain
site;
\item[(v)] the same formulas continue to the unbroken phase
($\tilde\kappa\to \ii\kappa$, i.e.\ $\kappa=\sqrt{s^{2}-\sin^{2}\theta}$), where
$1-2r^{2}=1-2(s/\kappa)^{2}\sin^{2}\kappa t$ with $(s/\kappa)^{2}>1$
oscillates through zero forever (recurrent indivisibility windows of
period $\pi/\kappa$), and to the exceptional point, where everything is
polynomial with $r=st$ and $t_c=1/(s\sqrt2)$, continuously in $s$.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $A=\ii\sin\theta\,\sigma_z+s\sigma_x$ obeys
$A^{2}=(s^{2}-\sin^{2}\theta)\mathbf 1=-\tilde\kappa^{2}\mathbf 1$,
$\ee^{-\ii At}=c\,\mathbf 1-\ii\sigma A$, giving
\eqref{eq:gredclosed} entrywise. With $r'=sc$ and
$p'=\tilde\kappa^{2}\sigma+\sin\theta\,c$ one finds the Wronskian-type
identities
\[
r'p-p'r \;=\; r'q-q'r \;=\; s\,(c^{2}-\tilde\kappa^{2}\sigma^{2}) \;=\; s ,
\]
constants in $t$; inserting them into
$L=\dot{\tilde\Gam}\tilde\Gam^{-1}-2\sin\theta\,\mathbf 1$ (the scalar
factor shifts only the diagonal) yields \eqref{eq:generator}; the
reduced divisor stays sub-stochastic, $\mathbf 1^{T}L=(0,-4\sin\theta)\le0$
for all $t$. Statements
(i)--(v) follow by inspection of signs: $p,r>0$ always, $1-2r^{2}$ crosses
zero once (hyperbolic) at $t_c$, $q$ crosses zero once at $t_0>t_c$; for
(iii), $2spr+(\sin\theta+\tilde\kappa)(1-2r^{2})
=2s^{2}\sigma(c-\tilde\kappa\sigma)+\sin\theta+\tilde\kappa
=2s^{2}\sigma\,\ee^{-\tilde\kappa t}+\sin\theta+\tilde\kappa>0$ while
$1-2r^{2}<0$ for $t>t_c$, so $L_{12}<-(\sin\theta+\tilde\kappa)$ there, and
$L_{12}\to -(\sin\theta+\tilde\kappa)$ from the asymptotics
$c\sim\tilde\kappa\sigma\sim\tfrac12\ee^{\tilde\kappa t}$.
\end{proof}

The predictions are exact against brute-force numerics
(Fig.~\ref{fig:generator}; closed forms to $10^{-14}$, generator to
$10^{-11}$; the observed floor $-1.207$ at $s=0.5$ is
$-(\sin\tfrac\pi4+0.5)=-1.2071$; the observed first indivisible time
$1.35$ on a $0.05$ grid is $t_c=1.3170$).

$L(t)=\dot\Gam_{\rm red}\Gam_{\rm red}^{-1}$ of the reduced process

\subsection{The two non-Markovianities dissociate}

For open systems, non-Markovianity is commonly diagnosed by information
backflow (increase of trace/total-variation distance between two
evolutions) \cite{BLP2009,RHP2010,BreuerReview2016}. On the classical
marginal with the leak recorded as a third outcome, the total-variation
distance is numerically monotone in \emph{both} phases (no classical
backflow observed, though with divisibility lost after $t_c$ monotonicity is no longer guaranteed);
on the survival-conditioned (post-selected) process, the late-window
backflow vanishes for every broken $s$ whose indivisibility onset $t_c(s)$
precedes the window (all $s\gtrsim10^{-6}$ here; conditional backflow in the
broken phase is confined to a neighborhood of $t_c(s)$, which grows as
$\tilde\kappa^{-1}\ln(\sqrt2\,\tilde\kappa/s)$ only as $s\to0$) and jumps at
the EP (e.g.\ $0.000\to1.32$ across $s_*=0.7071$ at $s=0.72$, window
$t\in[20,40]$)---the stochastic-level analogue of the
reversible--irreversible criticality of information flow established for
$\PT$ systems by Kawabata, Ashida, and Ueda \cite{KAU2017}. Meanwhile,
Barandes indivisibility is present in \emph{both}
phases; what changes at the EP is its temporal structure, recurrent
$\leftrightarrow$ terminal (the terminal-indivisibility theorem,
Theorem~\ref{thm:terminal}). Figure
\ref{fig:dissociation} contrasts the two diagnostics across the
transition: the backflow order parameter switches at the EP, while the
indivisibility onset $t_c(s)$ is finite and \emph{continuous} through it.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figs/fig_dissociation.pdf}
\caption{The two non-Markovianities across the $\PT$ transition
(EP at $s_*=\sin\tfrac\pi4\approx0.707$, dotted line). Left: conditional
total-variation backflow in an early ($t\in[0,20]$) and a late
($t\in[20,40]$) window; the late-window backflow vanishes in the broken
phase (for onsets $t_c(s)$ preceding the window) and switches on at the EP (the apparent smoothness just
above $s_*$ is the diverging Rabi period $\pi/\kappa$ meeting the finite
window). Right: the first indivisible time $t_c(s)$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:terminal}, analytic in both phases and continuous
through the EP (circles: numerical onset). Indivisibility is present on
both sides; backflow is not.}
\label{fig:dissociation}
\end{figure}

Thus the broken phase is \emph{backflow-Markovian in the late window
yet permanently Barandes-indivisible}:
the two notions genuinely dissociate, indivisibility being strictly finer.
That the relation between divisibility- and information-flow-based
notions of Markovianity is subtle for quantum channels---equivalent
for invertible maps \cite{ChruscinskiRS2018}, genuinely inequivalent
for noninvertible ones \cite{Rivas2022}---is known; the $\PT$ dimer
makes the dissociation exact,
phase-locked, and---in the broken phase---permanent, at the level of the
classical configuration process.
A closed Hermitian system, being quasi-periodic, can only be recurrently
indivisible; terminal indivisibility with monotone leak is the stochastic
signature of the broken phase. (At $n=2$ every lossy two-site Hamiltonian
\emph{with degenerate site frequencies and reciprocal coupling} is a shifted $\PT$ dimer, so
there ``overdamped open dynamics'' and ``broken
$\PT$'' coincide; a detuned pair adds a real $\sigma_z$ component the
$\PT$ form does not carry.)

\section{The exceptional point is a coordinate singularity}
\label{sec:ep}

At $s_*=\sin\theta$ the dimer is defective (a Jordan block; numerically
$\mathrm{rank}(H-\cos\theta\,\mathbf 1)=1$), the naive process grows
polynomially ($|U|^2$ column sums $\sim t^{2}$; fitted exponent $1.95$),
and the two-dimensional space of Hermitian intertwiners consists of one
\emph{singular positive-semidefinite rank-one ray} and one indefinite
direction: the positive-definite cone is exited exactly through its
boundary, and $\rho$ does not exist.

Approaching from the unbroken side, $s=s_*(1+\varepsilon)$,
$\kappa\simeq\sin\theta\sqrt{2\varepsilon}$, we find clean power laws
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ep}; log--log fits over $\varepsilon=10^{-1}\dots10^{-7}$,
all within $\pm0.0025$):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
quantity & scaling & fitted slope\\
\midrule
$\mathrm{cond}(\eta)$ & $\varepsilon^{-1}\sim\kappa^{-2}$ (Petermann
\cite{Petermann1979}) & $-0.998$\\
$\mathrm{cond}(\rho)$ (beable-map anisotropy) & $\varepsilon^{-1/2}\sim\kappa^{-1}$ & $-0.499$\\
$\norm{\Gam_{\varepsilon}(t)-\mathbf 1}$ at fixed $t$ & $\varepsilon\sim(\kappa t)^{2}$ & $+0.999$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.55\linewidth]{figs/fig_ep_scaling.pdf}
\caption{Collapse of the closed-system (metric) description approaching
the exceptional point from the unbroken side, $s=s_*(1+\varepsilon)$. The
metric condition number diverges with the Petermann scaling
$\varepsilon^{-1}\sim\kappa^{-2}$, the beable-map anisotropy as
$\varepsilon^{-1/2}\sim\kappa^{-1}$, while the metric-corrected process at
fixed laboratory time \emph{freezes},
$\norm{\Gam_\varepsilon(t)-\mathbf 1}\sim\varepsilon\sim(\kappa t)^2$.}
\label{fig:ep}
\end{figure}

At fixed laboratory time the metric-corrected process therefore
\emph{freezes}: $h\to\cos\theta\,\mathbf 1$ and
$\Gam_\varepsilon(t)\to\mathbf 1$. A regularized lab-time limit exists but
is trivial---and is not the EP dynamics, which grows like $t^{2}$. On the
critically rescaled clock $\tau=\kappa t$ the process converges (to
$10^{-10}$ between $\varepsilon=10^{-4}$ and $10^{-6}$) to the universal
indivisible Rabi process
\begin{equation}
\Gam_{\lim}(\tau)=
\begin{pmatrix}\cos^{2}\tau & \sin^{2}\tau\\ \sin^{2}\tau & \cos^{2}\tau
\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
with Rabi axis exactly $\sigma_x$; but the beable map identifying its
states with the original configurations diverges
($\mathrm{cond}\,\rho\sim\kappa^{-1}$): the survivor is abstract.

By contrast, every open-system quantity of Sec.~\ref{sec:broken}
(reduced-process entries, survival, indivisibility generator floor) varies
smoothly and monotonically through $s_*$. \emph{The EP is a singularity of
the similarity-to-Hermitian dictionary, not of the stochastic process}:
existence of an indivisible process is governed by the dilation, and the
deflation of Proposition~\ref{prop:deflation} fails at the EP only because
its coordinates do.

\part{The ghost problem}

\section{The Pais--Uhlenbeck oscillator}
\label{sec:pu}

The PU Lagrangian
$L=\tfrac{\gamma}{2}\bigl[\ddot x^{2}-(\omega_1^{2}+\omega_2^{2})\dot
x^{2}+\omega_1^{2}\omega_2^{2}x^{2}\bigr]$
has the Ostrogradski Hamiltonian (with $q_1=x$, $q_2=\dot x$)
\begin{equation}
H_{\rm PU}=p_1q_2+\frac{p_2^{2}}{2\gamma}
+\frac{\gamma}{2}(\omega_1^{2}+\omega_2^{2})q_2^{2}
-\frac{\gamma}{2}\omega_1^{2}\omega_2^{2}q_1^{2},
\label{eq:ostro}
\end{equation}
which is \emph{Hermitian} on the real configuration space $(x,\dot x)$,
with the ghost spectrum
$E=\omega_1(n_1+\tfrac12)-\omega_2(n_2+\tfrac12)$: real, unbounded below
\cite{PaisUhlenbeck1950,BenderMannheim2008}.

\subsection{The ghost realization is a valid fixed-beable process}

On a discretized $(x,\dot x)$ grid ($44^{2}=1936$ configurations, spectral
derivatives, half-width $L=10$, $\omega_1=1$, $\omega_2=0.5$, $\gamma=1$,
and---where a quartic term appears below---$\lambda=0.05$) the operator
\eqref{eq:ostro} is exactly Hermitian; $\Gam=|U|^{2}$ is doubly stochastic
to $2\times10^{-14}$ and genuinely indivisible (divisor \eqref{eq:divisor}
minimum entry $-24.55$; the family does not compose, $\norm{\Gam(2t)-\Gam(t)^{2}}\approx2.7$); Hermiticity makes (K1)--(K4)
hold trivially with $\eta=\mathbf 1$, so the beables are fixed. The
realization is validated dynamically: Ehrenfest is exact for quadratic
Hamiltonians, and $\avg{x(t)}$ tracks the exact classical fourth-order
trajectory to $3\times10^{-3}$ over half the evolution window (residual
from packet tails at the box walls; Fig.~\ref{fig:pu}).

The spectrum of the grid operator
runs from $-26$ to $+140$ and is \emph{completely irrelevant to the
process}: at the free level the Ostrogradski ghost is an energy-accounting
pathology, invisible to the stochastic representation.

At equal frequencies ($\omega_{1}=\omega_{2}=0.75$; the PU exceptional
point) the classical normal-mode
matrix is defective (eigenvector condition number $\sim10^{8}$; secular
$t\sin\omega t$ growth), yet the Hermitian realization passes through
smoothly: $\avg{x(t)}$ tracks $2\cos\omega t+\omega t\sin\omega t$ to
$9\times10^{-4}$ and $\Gam$ stays exactly doubly stochastic---the
PU-scale confirmation of Sec.~\ref{sec:ep}.

\subsection{The no-ghost quantization exits the sample space}

Mannheim's positive spectrum requires re-quantizing the ghost mode with a
vacuum satisfying $b^{\dagger}\Omega=0$, i.e.\ $\Omega\propto\ee^{+y^{2}/2}$:
normalizable only on a rotated contour \cite{BenderMannheim2008}. This is
a \emph{domain change}, not a similarity (a similarity cannot flip the
spectrum), and it has no shadow at any truncation: the null vector of the
truncated $b$ is $\ket{0}$ at every $N$ (the true vacuum,
truncation-stable), while the null vector of the truncated $b^{\dagger}$
is $\ket{N-1}$, pinned to the truncation edge and escaping to infinite
energy; restricted to any fixed low-energy sector,
$\sigma_{\min}(b^{\dagger})=1$ exactly. The contour-rotation operator
$R=\ee^{(\pi/2)D}$, $D=\tfrac12(yp+py)=\tfrac{\ii}{2}(a^{\dagger2}-a^{2})$,
has $\log_{10}\mathrm{cond}(R_N)$ reaching $\approx53$ decades by $N=48$
(tail slope $\approx1.2$ decades per Fock level; the low levels grow
slower), so the would-be metric
$\sim(RR^{\dagger})^{-1}$ is unbounded with unbounded inverse: the
Sec.~\ref{sec:ep} collapse at every scale at once, with no
critical-rescaling survivor.

\emph{Verdict.} The stochastic--quantum correspondence and the $\PT$
no-ghost construction resolve the free ghost in incompatible currencies:
Barandes keeps real beables and pays in negative energies; Mannheim buys
positive energies and pays with the sample space itself. (A related
construction by Salvio and Strumia \cite{SalvioStrumia2016} obtains
positive energies and normalizable wave functions at the price of a
negative-norm configuration-space inner product---again incompatible with
a probability measure over configurations.) The dynamics-dependent beable
rotation of Sec.~\ref{sec:deflation} escalates, in the PU limit, to beable
\emph{non-existence}.

\section{Interacting ghosts: conservative, transient, explosive}
\label{sec:interacting}

Does the ghost instability, once interactions are switched on, become a
\emph{stochastic} pathology? The answer arrives in stages.

\subsection{Quadratic interactions: a valid process turns transient}

Consider the Hermitian cascade Hamiltonian
\begin{equation}
H=\omega_1 a^{\dagger}a-\omega_2 b^{\dagger}b
+g\,(a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}+ab),
\label{eq:cascade}
\end{equation}
whose normal twin has $+\omega_2 b^{\dagger}b$. The Heisenberg mode matrix
has eigenvalues
$\bar\omega\pm\sqrt{\delta^{2}/4-g^{2}}$ (ghost; $\delta=\omega_1-\omega_2$,
$\bar\omega=(\omega_1+\omega_2)/2$), so the ghost sign collapses the
instability threshold from the \emph{sum} frequency, $g>(\omega_1+\omega_2)/2$
(normal), to the \emph{difference} frequency, $g>|\omega_1-\omega_2|/2$:
zero at resonance. At resonance the vacuum cascades with the exact
two-mode-squeezing laws
\begin{equation}
\avg{n_1(t)}=\avg{n_2(t)}=\sinh^{2}(gt),
\qquad
P\bigl(n_1\le K\bigr)=1-\tanh^{2K+2}(gt)\;\longrightarrow\;0 ,
\label{eq:cascadelaws}
\end{equation}
both matched numerically to $\lesssim10^{-3}$ (four decimals for $t\le6$) in the resolved regime
($\langle n\rangle\lesssim$ a few, before the $34^2$ Fock truncation
sets in at the last tabulated time) (Fig.~\ref{fig:cascade};
Fock truncation $34^2$, $g=0.15$; detuned growth consistent with
$(g^{2}/\kappa_g^{2})\sinh^{2}\kappa_g t$, $\kappa_g=\sqrt{g^{2}-\delta^{2}/4}$),
while $\Gam$ remains doubly stochastic to $2\times10^{-14}$ and the energy
books stay exactly balanced ($E_{\rm normal}=+8.301$,
$E_{\rm ghost}=-8.301$ at $t=12$, where the $34^{2}$ truncation already
costs ${\sim}4\%$ against the exact $\sinh^{2}(gt)$; $\avg{H}=0$ is
conserved to $4\times10^{-15}$ throughout, and it is that balance, not
the individual energies, that the argument uses).

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figs/fig_cascade.pdf}
\caption{The quadratic ghost cascade \eqref{eq:cascade} at resonance
($\omega_1=\omega_2$, $g=0.15$): a valid, exactly doubly stochastic

\emph{Vacuum decay is a
configuration-space cascade with balanced energy books}: the process is
valid and indivisible but \emph{transient}---it permanently escapes every
finite region at rate $2g$---which is the first stage at which the ghost
becomes visible in the process's own currency.

\subsection{Malicious nonlinearities: finite-time escape}

Classically, the ghost pair
$\tfrac12(p_1^{2}+\omega_1^{2}x_1^{2})-\tfrac12(p_2^{2}+\omega_2^{2}x_2^{2})
+\lambda x_1^{2}x_2^{2}$ is mostly bounded at moderate amplitude (benign
in the sense of \cite{Smilga2005}), while the quartic PU,
\begin{equation}
\ddddot{x}+(\omega_1^{2}+\omega_2^{2})\ddot
x+\omega_1^{2}\omega_2^{2}x=\frac{4\lambda}{\gamma}x^{3},
\end{equation}
exhibits robust \emph{finite-time} blowup for both signs of $\lambda$ at
every amplitude tested ($t_*=3.3$--$6.9$); for $\lambda>0$ the dominant
balance is $x\sim A(t_*-t)^{-2}$, $A=\sqrt{30\gamma/\lambda}$ (the
$\lambda<0$ escape proceeds by a different mechanism, taken up in
Sec.~\ref{sec:multiD}). Quantum-mechanically,
a lattice can transport the accelerating wavefront only while the required
classical speed stays below the Nyquist momentum $\pi/\Delta x$; box-size
drifts of the leak onset correlate exactly with
$v_{\rm cl}(\text{edge})/k_{\max}=1.4,2.4,3.4$ and are lattice
confinement, not physics (a fact that itself echoes
Sec.~\ref{sec:dictionary}: a non-\esa{} operator has no faithful finite
truncation). In the resolved regime, quantum probability arrives at every
probed radius ($r\le7$; the $r=8$ probe is marginally super-Nyquist) at
times bounded by the classical finite-time law, with bulk
weight, while the free control never passes $r\approx7$.

In the standard vocabulary of stochastic processes, the hierarchy is:
free/normal-sign $=$ \emph{conservative}; ghost with quadratic (or benign
nonlinear) interaction $=$ \emph{transient}; ghost with malicious
nonlinearity $=$ \emph{explosive} (mass leaves the state space in finite
time)---the last verdict at the level of the classical mechanism (its
minimal escape process, Sec.~\ref{sec:dictionary}); the quantum operator
can resist (the $\lambda<0$ completion candidates of
Sec.~\ref{sec:multiD}). The Ostrogradski ghost is a recurrence-classification problem.
Complementary operator-theoretic and dynamical stability results for
interacting
ghosts---the global-stability classification program of Errasti
D\'iez, Gaset Rif\`a, and Staudt \cite{ErrastiDiez2024} and its
multidimensional classical-scattering extension by
Held \cite{Held2025}, conserved quantities with discrete
spectrum \cite{Deffayet2026},
and exact moment bounds \cite{EwasiukProfumo2026}---identify
interaction
structures in which the cascade is arrested; in the present language,
those are mechanisms enforcing recurrence (none of these works uses
the stochastic/recurrence currency itself).

\section{The explosion dictionary}
\label{sec:dictionary}

The mechanism of malicious escape is one-dimensional: motion along a
direction where the effective potential falls super-quadratically. For
this mechanism the correspondence between the three descriptions can be
made a theorem.

\begin{theorem}[explosion dictionary, 1D mechanism model]
\label{thm:dictionary}
Let $H=\tfrac12 p^{2}+V$ on the line with an escaping end
($V\to-\infty$ eventually monotonically and $C^{1}$ with
$|V'|\,|V|^{-3/2}$ bounded (the mechanism model), the standard regularity
under which the limit-\emph{point} criterion below applies; the
limit-circle leg~(2) additionally requires the $C^{2}$ error control
($|V''|\,|V|^{-3/2}$ integrable) stated there.
Rauch--Reed's one-dimensional counterexamples
\cite{RauchReed1973} show these hypotheses cannot be dropped), and
define the classical time of flight
$T(E)=\int^{\infty}\dd x/\sqrt{2(E-V(x))}$.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(1)] If $T=\infty$ (e.g.\ $V\gtrsim-Kx^{2}$), the end is limit
point (Levinson's monotone criterion \cite{CoddingtonLevinson}; the
power-law case also by \cite{ReedSimon2}), and---with the other end also
limit point (the non-escaping end confining, or a second escaping end
likewise at $T=\infty$, as in $V=-x^2$)---$H$ is \esa{} \cite{ReedSimon2},
there is a unique unitary $U(t)$
and a unique conservative process.
\item[(2)] If $T<\infty$ (e.g.\ $V\lesssim -Kx^{2+\epsilon}$) and $V\in C^{2}$
with $|V''|\,|V|^{-3/2}$ integrable (the added control of
Prop.~\ref{prop:revival}, automatic for the power-law potentials here),
the end is
limit circle \cite{ReedSimon2}, by the (WKB-asymptotic) identity
\begin{equation}
|\psi_{\pm}(x)|^{2}\;\approx\;\bigl[2(E-V(x))\bigr]^{-1/2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\int^{\infty}\!|\psi_{\pm}|^{2}\,\dd x \;\approx\; T :
\label{eq:onelineidentity}
\end{equation}
\emph{the deficiency solution's norm is, to WKB accuracy, the
classical time of flight}.\footnote{Here $\psi_{\pm}$ (and, in
Theorem~\ref{thm:app-separable}(iii), $u_{n}$) denote the $L^{2}$
solutions at the \emph{real} mechanism energy $E$; the genuine
deficiency vectors solve $(\tau\mp\ii)u=0$, and their WKB modulus
carries an extra factor
$\exp\!\bigl(\mp2\,\mathrm{Im}\!\int^{x}\!\sqrt{2(z-V)}\bigr)=e^{O(T)}$,
bounded whenever $T<\infty$. The limit-circle \emph{classification}
(finiteness of the integral) is therefore unaffected, while the
quantitative norm identity ``$\approx T$'' holds up to this bounded
($x$-independent, though $T$-dependent) factor.} This added control is what makes both the
limit-circle classification and its quantitative norm identity
rigorous---under $C^{1}$ boundedness alone we expect a monotone staircase
potential can have $T<\infty$ yet be limit point, so the smoothness
should not be dropped.
Consequently:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] there is no canonical unitary dynamics: self-adjoint extensions
form a $U(n)$ family for $n$ escaping ends ($U(1)$ for one, $U(2)$ for two, including
completions that transmit through infinity), i.e.\ boundary data at
infinity \cite{ReedSimon2};
\item[(b)] every extension is conservative but, semiclassically, returns
probability from infinity after the finite classical round trip; its spectrum is discrete
with local spacing, semiclassically,
$\Delta E\simeq\pi/T_{\rm cross}(E)$, $T_{\rm cross}$ the
classical traversal time;
\item[(c)] the regulator-independent object is the minimal (Feller)
semigroup \cite{Feller1952} of the \emph{escape diffusion}
$\dd X=\sqrt{2(E-V(X))}\circ(\dd t+\varepsilon\,\dd W)$---the classical flow
run with a Brownian-perturbed clock, whose speed measure (driftless variant) is twice the WKB
deficiency density of~\eqref{eq:onelineidentity} (Lemma~\ref{lem:feller})---a
sub-stochastic, \emph{explosive} process losing mass at the classical escape
time;
\item[(d)] one integral decides classical completeness, quantum essential
self-adjointness (Weyl \cite{Weyl1910}), and stochastic conservativity
(Feller boundary classification: $+\infty$ is regular---accessible---when
$T<\infty$ and natural when $T=\infty$; Lemma~\ref{lem:feller}).
\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

The rigorous inputs are Weyl's alternative and the limit-point/limit-circle
criteria of \cite{CoddingtonLevinson,ReedSimon2} (Thms.~X.7--X.10); the WKB norms and the
semiclassical spacing law are used at their standard validity.

The stochastic leg~(2)(c)--(d) is not carried by $H$ itself: in the
malicious case every self-adjoint extension has spectrum unbounded below, so
$\ee^{-tH}$ is not even bounded, let alone sub-Markov, and $V\to-\infty$ is
neither a drift nor a nonnegative killing rate; nor is a ground-state
($h$-)transform available, since $E-V\ge c>0$ on the escaping end makes
$u''=-2(E-V)u$ oscillatory there, with no eventually-positive solution. The
leg is carried instead by the diffusion whose scale and speed are read off
the classical flow.

\begin{lemma}[the stochastic leg: an escape diffusion]
\label{lem:feller}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:dictionary} (in fact only $V$
continuous with $V\to-\infty$ is used), fix $E$ and $x_{0}$ with
$\sup_{x\ge x_{0}}V<E$, and set $v(x)=\sqrt{2(E-V(x))}$,
$\tau(x)=\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\dd y/v(y)$, $T=T(E)=\lim_{x\to\infty}\tau(x)$. Let
$X$ be the escape diffusion
$\dd X=v(X)\circ(\dd t+\varepsilon\,\dd W)$ (Stratonovich, any
$\varepsilon>0$) or its driftless variant $\dd X=v(X)\circ\dd W$, reflected
at $x_{0}$ and minimal at $+\infty$, with explosion time
$\zeta=\inf\{t:X_{t-}=+\infty\}$ and minimal semigroup $(P^{\min}_{t})$; in
scale/speed form the driftless variant has $s(x)=\tau(x)$ and
$m(\dd x)=2\,\dd x/v(x)$, i.e.\ $m$ is twice the WKB deficiency density
of~\eqref{eq:onelineidentity}, and its scale mass at $+\infty$ is $T$. Then:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] $+\infty$ is a \emph{regular} boundary if $T<\infty$ and a
\emph{natural} boundary if $T=\infty$; it is never exit or entrance. For the
driftless variant the two Feller test integrals coincide,
$\Sigma=N=T^{2}$.
\item[(ii)] If $T<\infty$ then $\Prob_{x}(\zeta<\infty)=1$: $(P^{\min}_{t})$
is strictly sub-stochastic ($P^{\min}_{t}\mathbf 1<1$ for $t>0$), the mean
explosion time is $\EE_{x_{0}}[\zeta]=\Sigma$ ($=T^{2}$ driftless), and
$\zeta\to T$ almost surely as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ (drifted variant). If $T=\infty$ then
$(P^{\min}_{t})$ is conservative and, $+\infty$ being natural, unique.
\item[(iii)] Consequently one integral decides all three legs; the
diffusion's own Weyl classification agrees; and at the regular boundary
Feller's lateral conditions (absorbing, reflecting, elastic, sticky,
jump-in) parametrize the sub-Markov extensions of $(P^{\min}_{t})$---the
stochastic mirror of the $U(1)$ family of self-adjoint extensions in~(2)(a),
the conservative ones returning probability from infinity as in~(2)(b).
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}

The proof constructs the escape diffusion explicitly and reduces the Feller
boundary classification to the single classical integral $T(E)$ (in the
clock coordinate both variants are Brownian motion on $[0,T)$, and
$\Sigma=N=T^{2}$ for the driftless one); it is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:feller}.

Numerical verification (representative $V=-x^{4}$---the two-escaping-end
case, deficiency $(2,2)$, the $U(2)$ completions of~(2)(a)---and marginal control
$V=-x^{2}$, limit-point with deficiency $(0,0)$): the spacing law matches to $3.5\%$ or better in all eight
$(V,L)$ cases tested and to $0.3\%$ at the largest box for
$V=-x^{4}$ (Table~\ref{tab:spacing}); the eigenvalue nearest $E=10$
(a tracked-level probe, distinct from the spacing window at $E=11$)
sweeps
erratically with the wall position (no completion has an $L\to\infty$
limit) while the spacing is stable; a 1D absorbing-boundary evolution
(where the lattice barrier is beatable) absorbs $98.6$--$98.8\%$ of the
probability by $t=1$ \emph{independently of box size}, with onset
saturating toward the classical arrival ($T_{\rm esc}=0.4635$, released
from rest at $x=2$, i.e.\ $E=-16$;
Fig.~\ref{fig:explosion}, left); and two finite-box stand-ins for the
extreme completions $R=1,0$---Dirichlet wall versus periodic identification---both revive
$\approx95\%$ of the probability after the finite round trip but from
\emph{opposite sides} (reflection versus a $+\infty\to-\infty$

\section{The completions dictionary: self-adjoint extensions and Feller lateral conditions}
\label{sec:completions}

Theorem~\ref{thm:dictionary}(2)(a)--(b) left the conservative physics of a
malicious end distributed over a family of completions---a $U(n)$ of
boundary conditions at infinity for $n$ escaping ends ($U(1)$ for one, a $U(2)$ for two),
including the completions that transmit through infinity (the
``wormhole'' of Fig.~\ref{fig:explosion}, right)---while
Lemma~\ref{lem:feller}(iii) recorded the parallel fact on the stochastic
side: at the regular boundary, Feller's lateral conditions parametrize
the sub-Markov extensions of the minimal semigroup. We called the second
family the stochastic mirror of the first. This section makes the mirror
a theorem and, in doing so, determines exactly what kind of mirror it
is. It is \emph{not} a bijection, and should not be read as one: the
correspondence is a surjection of the unitary family onto a small,
exactly characterized slice of the Feller--Wentzell cone, with the
quantum phases collapsing fiberwise. The two-sided failure of
bijectivity is itself the physics---the quantum side carries phase
(interference/spectral) data invisible to probability, and the
stochastic side carries dissipative and boundary-mass data forbidden to
unitarity. Everything at the class level below is exact, by limit-circle
boundary-form algebra whose only WKB input is the Olver-normalized
in/out frame that pins the boundary basis (App.~\ref{app:completions});
only the wave-packet arrival/revival interpretation is semiclassical, and
it is flagged as such there.

Fix the mechanism energy $E$---the same $E$ at which the escape
diffusion of Lemma~\ref{lem:feller} is built---and let $n\in\{1,2\}$
count the escaping ends with finite time of flight ($n=1$: the other end
confining, hence limit point with no boundary data, deficiency $(1,1)$;
$n=2$: e.g.\ $V=-x^{4}$, the deficiency-$(2,2)$ case of the numerics).
At each escaping end the solutions of $\tau u=Eu$ carry a conserved
probability current of signature $(1,1)$, splitting each germ into a
current-normalized \emph{incident} amplitude $a_{i}$ and a
\emph{returned} amplitude $b_{i}$. The first result
(Proposition~\ref{prop:bdyS}, Appendix~\ref{app:completions}) is that
for every self-adjoint extension $\tilde H$ the admissible germs at
energy $E$ satisfy $b=S_{\tilde H}(E)\,a$ for a unique unitary
$S_{\tilde H}(E)\in U(n)$, and $\tilde H\mapsto S_{\tilde H}(E)$ is a
\emph{bijection} from the self-adjoint extensions onto $U(n)$: the
completion literally is a boundary $S$-matrix, the $n$-channel instance
of the $S$-matrix at infinity of Sec.~\ref{sec:multiD}. Probability,
however, can see only the flux shadow of $S(E)$: the \emph{flux functor}
$\Phi_{E}$ (Definition~\ref{def:fluxfunctor}) assigns to $\tilde H$ the
lateral datum ``reflect at end $i$ with weight $|S_{ii}(E)|^{2}$, jump
to the other end with weight $|S_{ij}(E)|^{2}$; no killing, no
stickiness.''

\begin{theorem}[extension--lateral dictionary; $n\in\{1,2\}$ escaping
ends, $T_{i}(E)<\infty$, Olver-frame hypothesis of
App.~\ref{app:completions}; class level proved]
\label{thm:extlat}
Let $\mathcal C$ be the set of Feller--Wentzell lateral data
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:lateral}; one datum per escaping end) that are
simultaneously \emph{(c1) conservative} (no killing),
\emph{(c2) instantaneous} (no boundary stickiness),
\emph{(c3) boundary-supported} (the jump measure charges only the other
escaping end, never the interior), and \emph{(c4) reciprocal} (equal
passage weights in the two directions). Then:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(1)] \textbf{(image)} $\Phi_{E}$ maps the self-adjoint extensions
of $H$ \emph{onto} $\mathcal C$ and \emph{only into} $\mathcal C$; the
correspondence is not a bijection in either direction.
\item[(2)] \textbf{($n=1$: total collapse)} $\mathcal C$ is a single
point---pure reflection, $\dd u/\dd s\,(+\infty)=0$---and $\Phi_{E}$ is
the \emph{constant} map: the entire $U(1)$ of completions has one
stochastic shadow. The phase fixes the eigenvalue positions
(interference data) and has no sub-Markov counterpart.
\item[(3)] \textbf{($n=2$: a segment)} $\mathcal C\cong[0,1]\ni
R=|S_{11}(E)|^{2}$: reflect with weight $R$, pass through infinity
($+\infty\to-\infty$ and $-\infty\to+\infty$) with weight $1-R$,
necessarily the \emph{same} $R$ at both ends. $R=1$ are the separated
completions (e.g.\ the Dirichlet wall); $R=0$ are the wormhole/gluing
completions of Theorem~\ref{thm:dictionary}(2)(a), whose stochastic
shadow is Feller's circle-type passage: the jump-in datum linking
$+\infty$ to $-\infty$ (the ``darning'' of the two boundary points).
The fiber over each $R$ is a phase torus generically of dimension $3$
($\dim U(2)=4$ against $\dim\mathcal C=1$).
\item[(4)] \textbf{(sub-unitary $\leftrightarrow$ sub-Markov)} The flux
functor lands in $\mathcal C$ ($\operatorname{im}\Phi_{E}\subseteq\mathcal C$);
conversely, no lateral datum \emph{outside} $\mathcal C$ admits a
correspondence to a self-adjoint extension that preserves total probability,
the state-space measure class, and the boundary flux data, each obstruction
enforced by an exact invariant (Appendix~\ref{app:completions}). In particular, killing
(absorbing, elastic) is matched not by self-adjoint but by
\emph{maximal-dissipative} completions: the contractive boundary data
form the closed matrix ball $\norm{S(E)}\le1$, of which the self-adjoint
family is its unitary part---the extreme points (for $n=1$, the topological
boundary); $S=0$ (the outgoing/radiation condition
$b=0$) is the absorbing datum---precisely the minimal explosive
semigroup of Theorem~\ref{thm:dictionary}(2)(c), the completion
\emph{missing} from the self-adjoint family---and $0<\norm{S}<1$ are the
elastic data. Sticky and interior-jump data have no counterpart in
$L^{2}(\dd x)$ at all.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

The proof, entirely boundary-form algebra, is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:completions}, together with the full parametrization
and the four exclusions each tied to its invariant.

The $n=1$ collapse is structural, not an artifact of the map chosen: on
the escaping end $v\ge v_{\min}>0$, so the quantum phase
$\int^{\infty}v\,\dd x$ diverges while the clock
$\int^{\infty}\dd x/v=T$ converges. The quantum boundary basis winds
infinitely often approaching the end, whereas the diffusion's harmonic
boundary basis $(1,s)$ is monotone with finite limits; no linear
identification of the two boundary spaces can be canonical, and the only
basis-independent quantities transferable to the probabilistic side are
the current moduli $|S_{ij}|^{2}$. The phase is precisely the datum the
$|\cdot|^{2}$ functor forgets (Remark~\ref{rem:phasecollapse}).

One caveat deserves the main text rather than a footnote: the dictionary
is a correspondence between two \emph{parallel parametrized
families}---completions of $H$ in $L^{2}(I,\dd x)$ on one side, lateral
data of the escape diffusion on the other---a structural mirror, not an
isomorphism of spaces or of dynamics. No Hilbert-space map between the
two evolutions is asserted or possible; what transfers is exactly the
flux/class data, and Theorem~\ref{thm:extlat} says this transfer is onto
the slice $\mathcal C$ and nothing more. Likewise the dictionary is
stated at the mechanism energy $E$, the same $E$ at which the diffusion
itself is defined (a matching of reference energies intrinsic to the
construction); for a fixed completion the weight $R$ is generically
$E$-dependent, the separated and smooth-gluing families being the
$E$-uniform exceptions (Remark~\ref{rem:Ecovariance}), and at
$\varepsilon>0$ the reflect/jump weights are an excursion-rate
convention, literal per-arrival probabilities only in the transport
limit $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ (Remark~\ref{rem:ratenorm}).

The revival numerics of Fig.~\ref{fig:explosion} (right) are the two
extreme points of the segment: the Dirichlet wall is $R=1$ (return from
the right), the periodic identification is $R=0$ (return from the left,
through the wormhole), and intermediate completions split the returning
probability with weights $R$ and $1-R$ at the same finite round trip.
That wave-packet statement is the semiclassical leg of the
dictionary---the only leg that is
(Proposition~\ref{prop:revival}, \emph{[proved, sketch]}).

\section{Multidimensional theory: channels and the \texorpdfstring{$S$}{S}-matrix at infinity}
\label{sec:multiD}

The naive dictionary ``classical incompleteness
$\Leftrightarrow$ quantum non-self-adjointness'' fails in general
beyond the monotone one-dimensional mechanism model: Rauch and Reed
constructed one-dimensional counterexamples in both directions
\cite{RauchReed1973}, and in $n\ge2$ the correct structure is
channel-wise. Throughout,
$h_{\perp}$ has $C_0^{\infty}$ in its domain (as for any Schr\"odinger-type
transverse operator), $H$ is defined on $C_0^{\infty}$, and deficiency indices refer to its
closure.

\begin{theorem}[quadratic ghosts are safe]
\label{thm:quadratic}
Every at-most-quadratic Hamiltonian on $\mathbb R^{n}$---including the
indefinite cascade \eqref{eq:cascade} at any coupling, even in the
unstable phase, and the free PU operator \eqref{eq:ostro}---is \esa{} on
the span of Hermite functions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A quadratic $H$ maps Hermite level $N$ into levels $\le N+2$ with
coefficients $O(N)$, so
$\norm{H^{n}\psi_N}\le(2C)^{n}\,(N/2+n)!/(N/2)!$
and $\sum_n t^{n}\norm{H^{n}\psi_N}/n!$ converges for small $t$: every
Hermite function is an analytic vector. Nelson's analytic vector theorem
\cite{Nelson1959} applies.
\end{proof}

Thus instability never breaks uniqueness or conservativity: the
transient/explosive dichotomy of Sec.~\ref{sec:interacting} is rigorous on
the transient side.

\begin{theorem}[channel additivity of deficiency]
\label{thm:channels}
Let $H=h_1\otimes\mathbf 1+\mathbf 1\otimes h_{\perp}$ on
$L^{2}(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^{m})$, with $h_1=\tfrac12p^{2}+V_1$
limit circle at both ends $\pm\infty$ (e.g.\ $V_1$ super-quadratically
falling with the $C^{2}$ error-control of Theorem~\ref{thm:dictionary})
and $h_{\perp}$ self-adjoint
with orthonormal eigenbasis $\{\varphi_n,E_n\}$. Then for every $n$ and
every solution $u$ of $\bigl(h_1-(\pm\ii-E_n)\bigr)u=0$ (all of which are
$L^{2}$, since the limit-circle property at each end is independent of
the spectral
parameter), $u\otimes\varphi_n\in\ker(H^{*}\mp\ii)$. Hence the deficiency
indices of $H$ are $(\infty,\infty)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$H^{*}$ acts distributionally, and
$H(u\otimes\varphi_n)
=\bigl((\pm\ii-E_n)u\bigr)\otimes\varphi_n+E_n\,u\otimes\varphi_n
=\pm\ii\,u\otimes\varphi_n\in L^{2}$,
so $u\otimes\varphi_n\in D(H^{*})$ and is a deficiency vector; vectors
with different $n$ are orthogonal.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:central}
$-\tfrac12\Delta-c|x|^{\alpha}$ on $\mathbb R^{d}$, $d\ge2$, with $c>0$,
$\alpha>2$ has
deficiency indices $(\infty,\infty)$ (the same channel argument as
Theorem~\ref{thm:channels}, now applied to the angular-momentum
decomposition rather than a literal tensor sum: each fixed-$\ell$ radial
channel is limit circle at infinity---the super-quadratic $-c|x|^{\alpha}$
fall governs each end ($C^{2}$ automatic for the power law), the subquadratic
centrifugal term not spoiling it---and there are infinitely many channels).
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[non-separable channel mixing]
\label{thm:kato}
Let $H_0=\tfrac12p_2^{2}-|x_2|^{\alpha}+h_{\perp}(x_1)$, $\alpha>2$, with
$h_{\perp}$ having orthonormal eigenbasis as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:channels} (so that $H_0$'s indices are infinite), and
$B=\mu f(x_1)g(x_2)$ with $f,g$ bounded and real-valued. Then
$H_0+B$ has the same
(infinite) deficiency indices as $H_0$, while $B$ genuinely mixes the
transverse channels.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Bounded symmetric perturbations preserve deficiency indices
\cite{KatoBook}.
\end{proof}

\emph{Physical content.} The conservative completions of a malicious
multidimensional ghost are parametrized by unitaries between
infinite-dimensional deficiency spaces: a genuine \emph{$S$-matrix at
infinity}, which may scramble the transverse state upon ``reflection from
infinity''. Numerically (four-channel model, $V_1=-|x|^{5/2}$, bounded
mixing): the wall-regularized level count in a window matches the channel
sum $\sum_n\int T_{\rm cross}(E-E_n,L)\,\dd E/\pi$ (34 vs 32.3 at $L=8$;
37 vs 37.1 at $L=12$), at $\mu=0$ the spectrum is exactly a union of
shifted 1D ladders (deviation $10^{-12}$), switching on the bounded
coupling leaves the counts essentially unchanged ($34\to34$ at $L=8$,
$37\to36$ at $L=12$, a one-level window-edge shift within count
granularity; Kato stability made visible)---and a
packet sent to infinity in the transverse ground state returns as a
channel mixture \emph{whose content depends on where infinity was
regularized} (Fig.~\ref{fig:channels}): fractions
$(0.583,0.346,0.058,0.014)$ for $L=6$ versus $(0.401,0.421,0.143,0.035)$
for $L=9$, with $96\%/93\%$ of the probability returning at the classical
round-trip times.

\begin{remark}[an obstruction specific to ghosts]
For indefinite $H_0=h_1\otimes\mathbf 1+\mathbf 1\otimes h_\perp$ with
$h_1$ unbounded below, transverse observables are \emph{not}
$H_0$-bounded: joint spectral points $(E_1,E_\perp)$ with
$E_1\approx-E_\perp$ make $\norm{H_0\psi}$ small while
$\norm{h_\perp\psi}$ is large. The standard Kato--Rellich transfer
therefore fails even for mildly unbounded couplings---a second,
ghost-specific obstruction beyond non-separability.
\end{remark}

\begin{openproblem}
\label{op:pu}
Determine the deficiency indices of the exact quartic Pais--Uhlenbeck
operator, $H_{\rm PU}+\lambda q_1^{4}$ of \eqref{eq:ostro}, and of the
ghost pair with coupling $\lambda x_1^{2}x_2^{2}$, on
$C_0^{\infty}(\mathbb R^{2})$. Both escape the hypotheses above (unbounded
diagonal coupling; degenerate/indefinite kinetic form). Either outcome is
significant: infinite deficiency would complete the explosion dictionary
for the physical operators, while essential self-adjointness would be a
``quantum completion'' in the sense of \cite{RauchReed1973}---interactions
curing the ghost's explosion.
\end{openproblem}

\emph{Numerical evidence.} A regulator-dependence campaign (Fock-space
compressions, which regulate position and momentum symmetrically---the PU
escape accelerates without bound---with the matched-eigenvalue-drift
diagnostic calibrated on limit-circle, continuous-spectrum, and discrete
essentially-self-adjoint controls, and on the free PU operator as the
two-dimensional baseline) points to a split verdict. The quartic PU shows
persistent $O(1)$ drift that does not decay with truncation, at two
different basis scales---the limit-circle signature, at $\sim$5$\times$
the free-PU baseline: evidence \emph{against} essential self-adjointness,
i.e.\ for the explosion dictionary applying to the physical operator. The
ghost pair with $\lambda>0$ sits at the essentially-self-adjoint baseline,
as predicted by its Born--Oppenheimer channels
$W_n(x_2)=\tfrac12\omega_2^{2}x_2^{2}-(n+\tfrac12)
\sqrt{\omega_1^{2}+2\lambda x_2^{2}}$, all of which are confining.
Unexpectedly, so does the classically malicious $\lambda<0$ ghost
pair---tentative evidence of a genuine Rauch--Reed-type quantum
completion, in which zero-point energy walls off the narrow classical
escape valley. Evidence, not proof; but it sharpens the problem: prove
deficiency for the quartic PU by quasi-mode construction along its escape
channel, and test the quantum-completion hypothesis for the ghost pair
with valley-adapted bases. (Deficiency indices of related
polynomial-oscillator operators---higher-order squeezing---have recently
been computed rigorously, including cases where added terms
\emph{restore} essential self-adjointness \cite{FischerBL2026}: an
operator-theoretic precedent for completion by interaction.)

The drift verdict for the quartic PU can, in fact, be given structural
support, together with a falsifiable sign prediction:

\begin{proposition}[displaced-oscillator identity (exact) and
channelization (to $O(k^2)$) of the quartic PU]
\label{prop:bo}
In the Fourier-rotated representation ($p_1\to y$, $q_1\to-p_y$), the
displaced-oscillator unitary $U=\exp[-\ii\,y\,p_2/(\gamma\Omega)]$,
$\Omega=\omega_1^2+\omega_2^2$, gives \emph{exactly}
\begin{equation}
U\,(H_{\rm PU}+\lambda q_1^4)\,U^{\dagger}
=Q\!\Bigl(p_y+\frac{p_2}{\gamma\Omega}\Bigr)
+\frac{p_2^{2}}{2\gamma}+\frac{\gamma\Omega}{2}q_2^{2}
-\frac{y^{2}}{2\gamma\Omega},
\qquad
Q(p)=\lambda p^{4}-\frac{\gamma\Omega_0^{2}}{2}p^{2},
\end{equation}
with $\Omega_0^2=(\omega_1\omega_2)^2$ (the conjugating generator is bilinear, so
the Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff series terminates and the identity is exact;
confirmed numerically in Fock space to $10^{-9}$). The
mode-2-diagonal (channel) part is, in the $k=p_y$ representation and up
to $O(k^{2})$ corrections, \emph{minus} the Schr\"odinger operator
$\hat p^{2}/(2\gamma\Omega)-\lambda k^{4}+(\gamma\Omega_0^{2}/2)k^{2}$;
interchannel couplings are polynomial of degree $\le3$ in $k$,
subordinate to the quartic diagonal. Hence for $\lambda>0$ every channel
is limit circle (finite channel time of flight; the truncated window
$k=2\to400$ at $E_{0}=1$ gives $1.74$ for the parameters used here,
$\lambda=0.05$, against $2.06$ from the turning point to infinity), and
channel additivity as in Theorem~\ref{thm:channels}
argues for deficiency indices $(\infty,\infty)$---with the stability
question settled at every finite channel number by the finite-channel
stability theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:stability}) below. For
$\lambda<0$ every channel is (minus) a confining operator: quantum
completion predicted, \emph{despite} classical finite-time blowup at both
signs.
\end{proposition}

The sign prediction is confirmed by the drift diagnostic: the classically
explosive $\lambda<0$ quartic PU sits near the essentially-self-adjoint
baseline (ratios $0.17$--$0.46$ at two basis scales, measured over
$N=24$--$42$, none leaving
the essentially-self-adjoint baseline band), separated from the
$\lambda>0$ limit-circle signature
(the extended truncations give $O(1)$ ratios---$3.77$ at
$N=48{\to}56$, $1.06$ at $N=56{\to}64$; at the matched
$N=24$--$42$ range the $\lambda>0$ ratios are $0.79$--$1.96$, so the
separation is not an artifact of comparing disjoint windows---several times to an order
of magnitude
above the ${\sim}0.2$ \esa{} baseline; a single
early window at one basis scale dips to
$0.34$), while the $\lambda<0$ ghost pair
gives $0.49$ at $N=48{\to}56$ and $0.32$ at $N=56{\to}64$---the
first value at the upper edge of the essentially-self-adjoint band
(above the tighter 2D free-PU control) but both well below the
limit-circle signature. The $\lambda<0$ quartic PU thus joins the $\lambda<0$ ghost pair
as a Rauch--Reed-type quantum-completion candidate: in both cases the
classical escape appears to be walled off quantum mechanically, whereas
the $\lambda>0$ quartic PU escapes through momentum space along
limit-circle channels.

The stability of channel deficiency under the interchannel couplings---the
gap flagged after Proposition~\ref{prop:bo}---can in fact be closed at
every finite channel number, because the couplings are not generic
subordinate couplings: they are functions of the single operator $p_2$.

\begin{theorem}[finite-channel deficiency stability]
\label{thm:stability}
For every $M$, the $M$-channel Galerkin model of the channelized quartic
PU ($\lambda>0$),
\[
S_M=\frac{\hat p^{2}}{2\gamma\Omega}
-Q\bigl(k\,\mathbf 1+c\,P_{2,M}\bigr)
-\sqrt\Omega\,\bigl(N+\tfrac12\bigr)\Big|_{M},
\qquad c=\frac{1}{\gamma\Omega},
\]
with $P_{2,M}$ the truncated momentum of mode 2 ($S_M$ is the
channel operator of Proposition~\ref{prop:bo} taken with the
standard overall sign; $A\mapsto-A$ interchanges the deficiency
indices, and $S_M$ commutes with the natural conjugation of its decoupled
eigenframe (equivalently $K\circ\Pi_2$ in the mode-2 Fock basis), so
$n_+=n_-$ and the overall sign is immaterial), has deficiency indices
$(2M,2M)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$Q(k\mathbf 1+cP_{2,M})$ is a polynomial in the single Hermitian matrix
$P_{2,M}$, hence exactly diagonal in its constant ($k$-independent)
eigenframe; the eigenvalues $p_\nu$ of $P_{2,M}$ (scaled Gauss--Hermite
points) are simple. In that frame $S_M$ is a direct sum of the scalar
operators $\hat p^{2}/(2\gamma\Omega)-Q(k+c\,p_\nu)$---each limit circle
at both ends \cite{ReedSimon2}, contributing $(2,2)$---plus the rotated
channel-energy matrix, a \emph{constant bounded} Hermitian perturbation,
which preserves deficiency indices \cite{KatoBook}.
\end{proof}

The falsifiable consequence---with couplings on, the wall-regularized
level density must equal the all-$2M$-branch sum
$\sum_{\nu}T_{{\rm cross},\nu}/\pi$; were any channel's deficiency
destroyed, the density would drop---is confirmed numerically for
$M=1,2,3$ at three wall positions (count-based spacing matching the
prediction within count granularity; the exact frame decoupling holds to
$10^{-13}$, and the $\pm p_\nu$ branch ladders visibly lock into parity
doublets). What remains for the full operator is only the channel tail:
the exact Fock compression differs from the Galerkin model by terms
supported on the top four channels, and control of the deficiency
vectors' decay in channel number as $M\to\infty$ is open---equivalently,
the full operator is a direct integral of limit-circle fibers over
$\mathrm{spec}(p_2)$ coupled across fibers by the transverse oscillator.

\begin{corollary}[sign dichotomy at every finite channel number]
\label{cor:dichotomy}
For $\lambda<0$ the same frame decoupling yields the confining branches
$\hat p^{2}/(2\gamma\Omega)+|\lambda|(k+c\,p_\nu)^{4}$ (plus exact
lower-order quadratic terms, not a remainder), each
essentially self-adjoint; with the bounded rotated channel-energy term
(Kato--Rellich), every finite-channel Galerkin model of the $\lambda<0$
quartic PU is essentially self-adjoint. Thus at every $M$ the deficiency
indices are $(2M,2M)$ for $\lambda>0$ and $(0,0)$ for $\lambda<0$.
\end{corollary}

Numerically, the $\lambda<0$ wall-regularized spectra converge as the
wall recedes (matched drift $3\times10^{-8}$, then $10^{-13}$)---the
regulator-independence fingerprint, in maximal contrast to the
never-converging $\lambda>0$ sweep.

\emph{Mechanisms of the two completion candidates.} Tracking the actual
classical blowup trajectories and evaluating the kinetic form on the
escaping momentum discriminates the mechanisms sharply. The $\lambda>0$
PU (the deficiency case) escapes along a \emph{definite} kinetic
direction (form ratio $\to 0.9997$). The ghost pair's escape repeatedly returns to the
\emph{null} diagonal valley: at the four sampled amplitude-threshold
crossings the kinetic-form ratio $(p_1^{2}-p_2^{2})/(p_1^{2}+p_2^{2})$ is
small ($0.01$--$0.07$), but this is not a trajectory-wide bound---between
crossings the ratio oscillates across the full range---so the escape
never locks onto a definite kinetic direction the way the $\lambda>0$ PU
does ($\to0.9997$), supporting no sustained definite-kinetic WKB
transport, a structural rationale for its quantum completion. We conjectured that completion is equivalent to null escape;
the $\lambda<0$ PU \emph{falsifies} the naive form of this conjecture:
its ratio also tends to $1$. Inspection shows it climbs a \emph{rising}
potential using negative kinetic energy while hugging the line
$u=-cv$---that is, it escapes through arbitrarily high mode-2 channels,
precisely the channel tail excluded by the Galerkin models. The two
candidates therefore realize two distinct completion mechanisms
(null-direction escape versus channel-tail escape), the second more
tail-sensitive than the first. Finally, the ghost-pair verdict is robust
under valley-adapted bases: in rotated $(s,d)$ Fock bases aligned with
the escape diagonal, at three squeezing choices including strongly
valley-penetrating ones, the $\lambda<0$ drift ratios ($0.16$--$0.45$)
interleave with the free ($\lambda=0$) control's range---nothing
approaching the limit-circle band.

Finally, the $M\to\infty$ tail itself.

\begin{proposition}[direct-integral deficiency; the exact form of the
tail question]
\label{prop:directintegral}
The oscillator-free part $A_0=\hat p_k^{2}/(2\gamma\Omega)-Q(k+c\,p_2)$
commutes with $p_2$: it is a direct \emph{integral} over
$\mathrm{spec}(p_2)=\mathbb R$ of shifted scalar fibers, each limit
circle for $\lambda>0$, and measurable fields of fiber deficiency
solutions give $n_\pm(A_0)=\infty$. The full channelized operator differs
from $A_0$ by the transverse oscillator
$h_2=p^{2}/(2\gamma)-(\gamma\Omega/2)\partial_p^{2}$, which in this
exact frame is \emph{unbounded} (its Galerkin compressions have norm
$\sqrt\Omega(M-\tfrac12)\to\infty$)---precisely why the finite-channel
stability theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:stability}) does not pass trivially
to $M\to\infty$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[no compact witnesses]
\label{rem:nocompact}
For any symmetric operator defined on $C_0^{\infty}$ and any
$\mathrm{Im}\,z\ne0$, every compactly supported smooth $\psi$ lies in
the closure's domain, where
$\norm{(S-z)\psi}\ge|\mathrm{Im}\,z|\,\norm{\psi}$ holds
unconditionally. \emph{No compactly supported---windowed or
lattice-regularized---function can witness deficiency, nor any finite
basis (Fock/Hermite) truncation, which lies in the closure's domain by
a cutoff argument (the operators here have polynomially bounded coefficients)}:
the completion data lives entirely at infinity, in the same sense in
which the completions themselves are an $S$-matrix at infinity
(Theorem~\ref{thm:channels}). All truncation-based diagnostics,
including ours, are therefore evidence in principle, never proof;
genuine von Neumann certificates require candidates with analytically
controlled tails.
\end{remark}

Such a certificate is implementable in one dimension: gluing WKB tails
(whose only residual is the explicit remainder
$a[q''/(4q)-\tfrac{5}{16}(q'/q)^{2}]$, $q=(z-U)/a$ with $U$ the fiber
potential and $a$ its leading coefficient) to an exactly
integrated interior yields, for the falling-quartic fiber, a
machine-checked ratio $\norm{(S-\ii Z)\psi}/(Z\norm{\psi})=0.0011\ll1$:
the 1D fiber operator is \emph{certified}---in the computer-assisted
sense: the residual is evaluated numerically with a three-orders
safety margin, not rigorously error-bounded---not essentially
self-adjoint.
The construction is intrinsically sharp: for the confining control the
left continuation contains a non-$L^{2}$ real-exponential branch (no
candidate exists), and for the marginal falling-quadratic case the
growing branch is polynomial, $|\psi|\sim k^{-1/2+Z/1.12}$,
non-$L^{2}$ for every $Z>0$---consistent with the limit-point verdict
of Theorem~\ref{thm:dictionary}(1). On the full two-dimensional
operator, windowed candidates
supply a \emph{closure-defect diagnostic}: falling-channel operators hug
the forbidden bound (ratios $1.21$--$1.41$ for the five $\lambda>0$ beam
launches---four at $1.21$--$1.29$, the outer-window launch at
$1.41$---bracketing the 1D limit-circle control at $1.22$),
while the $\lambda<0$ control sits at $641$ and the 1D confining control
at $4.9$. The remaining mathematical content of the $M\to\infty$
question is thereby isolated: a two-dimensional certificate requires
analytic beam tails along the escape rays.

A Maslov feasibility study (\texttt{scripts/maslov\_feasibility.py}) indicates the
geometry of those tails. In the null-frame coordinates the ray equations
of the symbol reduce \emph{exactly} to the quartic-PU law
$s''''+\Omega s''+\Omega_0^{2}s=(4\lambda/\gamma)\,s^{3}$, so the
escape-ray family is the classical blowup itself, with universal
asymptotics $s\simeq A\,(t_*-t)^{-2}$, $A=\sqrt{30\gamma/\lambda}$:
measured exponent $-1.999$ and amplitude $24.55$ against
$\sqrt{600}=24.49$, at every launch point tested, in \emph{both} time
directions---rest data escapes at both temporal ends, so the
time-integral quasimode $\psi=\int\ee^{\ii z t}\varphi_t\,dt$ over a
doubly-explosive ray has only $\ee^{-Zt_*}\!\approx\!10^{-14}$ boundary
terms. Along these rays the Gaussian-beam data \cite{Ralston1982} are
favorable precisely where analytic tails are needed: Siegel positivity of the complex
curvature holds throughout, its numerical-zero direction aligned with
the flow (alignment $1.0000$; benign, integrated over in $t$); the
transverse curvature stays positive with strong terminal focusing
(width $5\times10^{-3}$ at the escape end); no caustics occur;
$|\det\partial q|\sim\tau^{-3.1}$ makes the beam-norm integral converge
($N=0.082$ at $Z=6$); and the anharmonic residual proxy decays rapidly
along the escape (weighted integral $0.026$), failing only in a
\emph{compact interior episode} of transverse defocusing (peak
$8\times10^{3}$)---exactly the region the one-dimensional template
already handles by exact numerical solution. The two-dimensional glued
certificate thus should reduce to: numeric interior on a compact region,
Gaussian-beam tails along the known rays, gluing where the residual
proxy is small---assembly and quantitative error control rather than
new asymptotic machinery.

\section{Experimental probes}
\label{sec:probes}

The closed forms of this paper are testable with existing devices;
we collect the concrete predictions.

\emph{(a) The terminal-indivisibility theorem in a lossy photonic
coupler.} The dimer \eqref{eq:dimer} is the standard system of
experimental $\PT$ optics (coupled waveguides or resonators with
engineered loss), and the correspondence's objects are normalized
intensities, so classical light suffices. The apparatus exists:
passive $\PT$ lossy couplers are characterized as functions of
propagation length \cite{Klauck2019}, information-flow criticality
at the exceptional point has been measured in single-photon dimers
\cite{Xiao2019}, and divisibility \emph{classes} (P- vs
CP-divisibility) are experimentally resolvable \cite{Bernardes2015}.
What has not been extracted is precisely this paper's observable
set: a closed-form divisibility-onset length, a generator floor,
and the recurrent-window period. Protocol: measure the
intensity transfer matrix $\Gam(t)$ at a sequence of propagation
lengths, form the divisor $M=\Gam(t_2)\Gam(t_1)^{-1}$
\eqref{eq:divisor}, and locate the onset of negativity.
Theorem~\ref{thm:terminal} predicts, with no free parameters beyond
the device constants $(\theta,s)$: onset at
$t_c=\tilde\kappa^{-1}\mathrm{arcsinh}(\tilde\kappa/(s\sqrt2))$,
\emph{permanent} thereafter in the broken phase, with the generator
floor $-(\sin\theta+\tilde\kappa)$; \emph{recurrent} windows of
period $\pi/\kappa$ in the unbroken phase; and $t_c$ continuous
through the exceptional point. The dissociation of
Sec.~\ref{sec:broken} is testable on the same data set by running
the two analyses (trace-distance backflow versus divisibility) side
by side: backflow dies at the exceptional point while the
indivisibility onset crosses it smoothly.

\emph{(b) The conditioning signal on an entangled pair.} Applying
the local lossy dimer to one arm of an entangled photon pair and
post-selecting on survival shifts the partner's marginal by a
closed-form amount with late-time magnitude
$\sqrt{1-(s/\sin\theta)^{2}}$---zero at the exceptional point,
approaching unity deep in the broken phase---while the
\emph{unconditioned} marginal is exactly unchanged (derivation in
the companion sequel; the closed
form follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:terminal}'s $p,q,r$). This is a direct laboratory
test of the sample-space bookkeeping that this paper argues is the
content of broken-$\PT$ physics, and it locates the apparent
no-signaling violation of local $\PT$ operations reported in the
literature in the post-selection, quantitatively. The
entangled-pair-plus-tailored-loss platform exists
\cite{Tang2016,Ehrhardt2022}; the closed-form magnitude is the new
target.

\emph{(c) The skin obstruction in circuits and lattices.}
Hatano--Nelson physics is routinely realized in topolectrical
circuits and photonic lattices; Theorem~\ref{thm:skin} identifies
the measured skin envelope with the beable metric,
$d_i=\ee^{2wi}$, and Corollary~\ref{cor:topology} predicts a
specific new experiment: two opposite-field chains coupled by
reciprocal rungs (the $\mathbb Z_2$-type ladder, net winding zero)
are obstructed at \emph{any} rung coupling---observable as
irreducible nonreciprocal transport that no local gauge
transformation removes. The $\mathbb Z_2$ skin effect itself has
recently been observed (acoustically and in programmable circuits
\cite{Z2experiments}); the prediction here is not its existence but
the \emph{beable-obstruction observable}: the irreducibility of the
nonreciprocity, equivalently the detailed-balance violation of the
underlying rate graph, which mode-localization measurements do not
extract.

\emph{(d) The explosion dictionary in engineered potentials.}
Level-spacing \emph{saturation}, $\Delta E=\pi/T_{\rm cross}$
approaching a finite limit as the box grows rather than decaying to
zero (Table~\ref{tab:spacing}), is the
spectroscopic fingerprint of reflection off infinity at finite
recurrence time (the underlying boundary-condition spectra are
classical mathematical physics \cite{BottomlessPotentials}; the
saturation framing as an observable appears new); engineered
super-quadratically falling potentials in cold-atom or circuit-QED
simulators could exhibit it directly---inverted-oscillator BEC
platforms with full state tomography now exist
\cite{InvertedHO2026} and are one nonlinearity away from the
$-x^{4}$ class. On the transient side, the cascade laws
\eqref{eq:cascadelaws} are two-mode squeezing, already measured as
vacuum pair creation in the dynamical Casimir effect
\cite{WilsonDCE}; engineered superlinearly Bose-enhanced ladders
would probe the transient-to-explosive transition itself.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What each program learns}

\emph{For the antilinearity program.} Closed-system $\PT$ machinery adds
no new stochastic processes: in the unbroken phase it is an isomorphism
onto ordinary quantum mechanics (the deflation proposition,
Proposition~\ref{prop:deflation}), at the
EP its coordinates degenerate (Sec.~\ref{sec:ep}), and its genuinely new
regime---the broken phase---is most naturally read as \emph{open-system}
physics: survival-conditioned dynamics whose stochastic signature is the
recurrent$\,\to\,$terminal transition of indivisibility
(Theorem~\ref{thm:terminal}). For PU, the no-ghost construction is domain
data at infinity rather than dynamics on the configuration space
(Sec.~\ref{sec:pu}); the explosion dictionary
(Theorem~\ref{thm:dictionary}(2)(a)) and the channel-additivity theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:channels}) show precisely what such data amounts to.

\emph{For the stochastic--quantum correspondence.} Antilinear symmetry is
the exact existence gate for closed processes; detailed balance
(the detailed-balance theorem, Theorem~\ref{thm:kolmogorov}) is the
exact fixed-beable gate, resolving
both conceptual costs on that class and giving the metric weights a
classical meaning (inverse stationary distribution). The correspondence
handles ghosts gracefully---too gracefully to see them at free level: the
ghost enters the stochastic currency only through recurrence
classification (conservative $\to$ transient $\to$ explosive), and the
minimal explosive process is the only completion-independent object.

\subsection{The trilemma}

For a malicious interacting ghost one must choose: (i)~accept the minimal
explosive process---sub-stochastic, the dilation structure of
Sec.~\ref{sec:broken}, canonical; (ii)~buy conservativity with an
$S$-matrix at infinity---in the separable multi-D model an infinite-dimensional,
channel-scrambling reflection law injected by hand
(Theorem~\ref{thm:channels}); or (iii)~repair the spectrum by moving the
domain---which exits the sample space (Sec.~\ref{sec:pu}). In Barandes'
currency, (i) is the answer; vacuum decay \emph{is} explosion in the
probabilist's standard sense.

\subsection{Outlook}

Open directions: Open Problem~\ref{op:pu}, whose two-dimensional
certificate should reduce to assembly and error control over the beam
data supplied in Sec.~\ref{sec:multiD}; field theory, where the
explosion/transience distinction should meet vacuum decay rates; the
interacting broken phase, where terminal indivisibility and transience
could combine; and extensions of the skin-effect correspondence of
Sec.~\ref{sec:skin} to higher-dimensional and symmetry-protected skin
effects \cite{Okuma2020}, where the obstruction should acquire the
corresponding topological classification.

\section{Proof of the escape-diffusion lemma}
\label{app:feller}

This appendix proves Lemma~\ref{lem:feller} (the stochastic leg of
Theorem~\ref{thm:dictionary}): for the escape diffusion
$\dd X=v(X)\circ(\dd t+\varepsilon\,\dd W)$ (or its driftless variant),
$v=\sqrt{2(E-V)}$, reflected at $x_{0}$ and minimal at $+\infty$, the
boundary $+\infty$ is regular iff $T(E)<\infty$ and natural iff
$T(E)=\infty$, and the minimal semigroup is sub-stochastic (explosive) in
the regular case.

Transport to the clock coordinate $\tau:[x_{0},\infty)\to[0,T)$, a $C^{1}$
increasing bijection ($v\ge v_{\min}=\sqrt{2(E-\sup V)}>0$); scale and speed
are intrinsic under such maps, and the images of the two diffusions are
Brownian motion on $[0,T)$ with drift $\beta\in\{0,1\}$ and variance
$\varepsilon^{2}$: $\dd\tilde s=\ee^{-\varrho u}\dd u$,
$\tilde m(\dd u)=\tfrac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}\ee^{\varrho u}\dd u$,
$\varrho=2\beta/\varepsilon^{2}$. With
$\Sigma=\int^{\infty}M\,\dd S$ and $N=\int^{\infty}S\,\dd M$ (boundary
classification of \cite{Feller1952,Feller1957}; \cite{KarlinTaylor},
Ch.~15.6; \cite{ItoMcKean}): for $T<\infty$ both $\tilde S(0,T)$ and
$\tilde M(0,T)$ are finite, so $\Sigma,N\le\tilde S\,\tilde M<\infty$ and
$+\infty$ is regular; the driftless case (unit noise $\varepsilon=1$) has $\tilde S(0,\xi]=\xi$,
$\tilde M(0,\xi]=2\xi$, whence $\Sigma=\int_{0}^{T}2\xi\,\dd\xi=T^{2}=N$
(a general $\varepsilon>0$ rescales $\Sigma,N$ by $\varepsilon^{-2}$ and
leaves the regular/natural dichotomy unchanged). For
$T=\infty$ both diverge, so $+\infty$ is natural; $\Sigma<\infty\Leftrightarrow
T<\infty\Leftrightarrow N<\infty$, so exit and entrance never occur, proving
(i); $E$-independence follows from $(E-V)/(E'-V)\to1$ as $x\to\infty$.

For (ii) with $T<\infty$, the reflected process hits $T$ almost surely
(Skorokhod: $Z_{t}\ge z+\beta t+\varepsilon W_{t}\to\infty$; reflecting
Brownian motion is recurrent when $\beta=0$), so $\zeta<\infty$ a.s., and as
$T$ is accessible (reachable with positive probability),
$P^{\min}_{t}\mathbf 1(x)=\Prob_{x}(\zeta>t)<1$. Solving $D_{m}D_{s}u=-1$
with $D_{s}u(x_{0})=0$ and $u(\infty)=0$ gives
$\EE_{x_{0}}[\zeta]=\Sigma$ (\cite{KarlinTaylor}, \S15.3); the small-noise
sandwich $t+\varepsilon W_{t}\le Z_{t}\le t+2\varepsilon\sup_{s\le t}|W_{s}|$
forces $\zeta\to T$ a.s.\ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ ($\beta=1$). For $T=\infty$, $\Sigma=\infty$ makes $+\infty$
inaccessible (Feller's explosion test), so $\zeta=\infty$ a.s.\ and
$P^{\min}_{t}\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1$; uniqueness at a natural boundary is
Feller's theorem, no lateral condition being admissible. Part~(iii) is the
classification of lateral conditions at a regular boundary
\cite{Feller1957,ItoMcKean}, matched to (2)(a)--(b). Finally, accessibility
forces $S<\infty$, whence $N\le S\cdot M<\infty$ whenever the speed mass is
finite: an accessible boundary honoring the WKB identity is regular, never
exit.

\section{The multidimensional escape dictionary}
\label{app:multiD}

This appendix extends the one-dimensional escape-diffusion leg of the
explosion dictionary (Theorem~\ref{thm:dictionary}(2)(c)--(d),
Lemma~\ref{lem:feller}) to the channel setting of
Sec.~\ref{sec:multiD}, providing the exact stochastic mirror of the
channel-additivity and bounded-mixing theorems
(Theorems~\ref{thm:channels} and~\ref{thm:kato}) and locating precisely
where the dictionary stops being a theorem. The verdict is
\emph{per-channel-only}: the dictionary generalizes verbatim channel by
channel in the separable, central, and boundedly mixed cases, and the
genuine obstruction is unbounded channel transfer---whose two failure
modes turn out to be asymmetric in exactly the way the paper's
numerical verdicts are (conservativity robust, explosivity
fragile; Theorem~\ref{thm:app-ghost}).

\emph{Status conventions.} Every statement below is tagged
\emph{[proved]}, \emph{[proved, sketch]} (rigorous reduction to cited
standard machinery, details routine but not written out), or
\emph{[conjecture]}. Lemma~\ref{lem:feller} is used throughout in the
form stated in Sec.~\ref{sec:dictionary} and proved in
Appendix~\ref{app:feller}: for the escape diffusion
$\dd X=v(X)\circ(\beta\,\dd t+\varepsilon\,\dd W)$,
$v=\sqrt{2(E-V)}$, $\beta\in\{0,1\}$, reflected at the base point and
minimal at $+\infty$, the boundary $+\infty$ is \emph{regular} iff the
classical time of flight $T=\int^{\infty}\dd x/v$ is finite (minimal
semigroup sub-stochastic and explosive) and \emph{natural} iff
$T=\infty$ (conservative, unique Feller realization), the driftless
speed measure being twice the WKB deficiency density
of~\eqref{eq:onelineidentity}.

\subsection{What replaces the single end and the single integral}
\label{app:multiD-setting}

In one dimension the escaping end is a point and one integral $T(E)$
decides all three legs. In the separable setting of
Theorem~\ref{thm:channels},
\[
H \;=\; h_{1}\otimes\mathbf 1+\mathbf 1\otimes h_{\perp}
\quad\text{on } L^{2}(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^{m}),
\qquad h_{1}=\tfrac12p^{2}+V_{1},\quad
h_{\perp}\varphi_{n}=E_{n}\varphi_{n},
\]
the classical escape mechanism conserves the transverse energy and the
quantum deficiency vectors are $u_{n}^{\pm}\otimes\varphi_{n}$. The
correct multidimensional replacements, established below, are:
\begin{itemize}
\item the single escaping end $+\infty$ becomes the \emph{countable set
of channel ends} $\{\infty_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb N}$, one per transverse
eigenstate (for continuous transverse spectrum, a measure space of
fiber ends; Sec.~\ref{app:multiD-tail});
\item the time of flight becomes the \emph{per-channel} family
\[
\begin{gathered}
T_{n}\;=\;T(E-E_{n})
\;=\;\int_{x_{n}}^{\infty}\frac{\dd x}{\sqrt{2\,(E-E_{n}-V_{1}(x))}},\\
x_{n}\ \text{any base point with }\sup_{x\ge x_{n}}V_{1}<E-E_{n};
\end{gathered}
\]
this is the precise form taken by a would-be direction-dependent
$T(E,\omega)$: the direction label $\omega$ is quantized into the
channel label $n$ (for central potentials, literally the angular
decomposition, Corollary~\ref{cor:app-central});
\item and---genuinely new in $d>1$---a \emph{second} kind of end, the
\emph{channel tail} $n\to\infty$ at finite or co-moving $x$, with no 1D
counterpart, which is exactly where the clean dictionary stops being a
theorem (Secs.~\ref{app:multiD-obstruction}--\ref{app:multiD-tail}).
\end{itemize}
Throughout, $V_{1}$ is continuous with $V_{1}(x)\to-\infty$; where the
quantum leg is invoked we assume in addition the regularity of
Theorem~\ref{thm:dictionary}, including its added $C^{2}$ control
($|V''|\,|V|^{-3/2}$ integrable, Prop.~\ref{prop:revival}). A first elementary but structurally
important fact:

\begin{lemma}[channel class equality; proved]\label{lem:app-classeq}
If $V_{1}(x)\to-\infty$ then for all $n,m$:
$T_{n}<\infty\iff T_{m}<\infty$. Hence the \emph{qualitative}
classification is channel-uniform; only the \emph{quantitative} data
$(T_{n})_{n}$ is channel-dependent.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\bigl(E-E_{n}-V_{1}(x)\bigr)/\bigl(E-E_{m}-V_{1}(x)\bigr)\to1$ as
$x\to\infty$, so the integrands are comparable near $+\infty$; the
finite parts are finite by continuity and the choice of base points.
\end{proof}

This is the stochastic shadow of the $\lambda$-independence of the
limit-point/limit-circle dichotomy used in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:channels}: there, every channel is limit circle
because one channel is; here, every channel end is regular because one
is.

\subsection{The separable case: full per-channel generalization}
\label{app:multiD-separable}

Define the state space as the disjoint union of half-lines
$\mathcal E=\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathbb N} I_{n}$, $I_{n}=[x_{n},\infty)$,
and on each $I_{n}$ the channel escape diffusion $X^{(n)}$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:feller} with speed
$v_{n}(x)=\sqrt{2(E-E_{n}-V_{1}(x))}$, clock
$\tau_{n}(x)=\int_{x_{n}}^{x}\dd y/v_{n}(y)$, reflection at $x_{n}$,
minimal (absorbing) convention at $\infty_{n}$; generator
$\mathcal L=\bigoplus_{n}L_{n}$,
$L_{n}=\tfrac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}(v_{n}\partial_{x})^{2}
+\beta\,v_{n}\partial_{x}$, $\beta\in\{0,1\}$. This is the stochastic
realization of the separable mechanism: the transverse motion is
bounded oscillation whose only dynamical residue is its conserved
energy, i.e.\ the frozen channel label.

\begin{theorem}[channel dictionary, separable case; proved except the
countable part of (iv)]\label{thm:app-separable}
Let $V_{1}$ be continuous, $V_{1}\to-\infty$, and let
$(E_{n},\varphi_{n})$ be as above. Then:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] \textbf{(per-channel classification)} Each channel end
$\infty_{n}$ is \emph{regular} iff $T_{n}<\infty$ and \emph{natural}
iff $T_{n}=\infty$ (never exit, never entrance); by
Lemma~\ref{lem:app-classeq} all channel ends are in the same class. For
$\beta=0$ (unit noise, $\varepsilon=1$) the Feller test integrals in channel $n$ are
$\Sigma_{n}=N_{n}=T_{n}^{2}$ (in general $T_{n}^{2}/\varepsilon^{2}$).
\item[(ii)] \textbf{(explosion)} If $T_{n}<\infty$ (equivalently, for
all $n$): started in channel $n$, the process explodes a.s.\ with mean
lifetime $T_{n}^{2}$ ($\beta=0$, $\varepsilon=1$) resp.\
$T_{n}-\tfrac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}
\bigl(1-\ee^{-2T_{n}/\varepsilon^{2}}\bigr)$ ($\beta=1$), and
$\zeta\to T_{n}$ a.s.\ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ ($\beta=1$): \emph{each channel
loses its mass at its own classical escape time}. The minimal
semigroup $\bigoplus_{n}P^{\min,n}_{t}$ is strictly sub-stochastic on
every channel. If $T_{n}=\infty$ for one (hence every) $n$, the direct
sum is conservative and is the unique Feller realization of
$\mathcal L$ with the given reflecting data.
\item[(iii)] \textbf{(deficiency $=$ speed-measure data, channel by
channel)} The driftless speed measure of channel $n$ is the WKB
density~\eqref{eq:onelineidentity} of the deficiency vector
$u_{n}\otimes\varphi_{n}$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:channels}:
$m_{n}(\dd x)=2[2(E-E_{n}-V_{1}(x))]^{-1/2}\dd x\approx
2\,|u_{n}^{\rm WKB}(x)|^{2}\,\dd x$, with scale mass $T_{n}$ near
$\infty_{n}$. Thus deficiency indices $(\infty,\infty)$ correspond
exactly to \emph{countably many boundary ends, each carrying finite
scale and speed mass}---one Feller datum per deficiency channel.
\item[(iv)] \textbf{(lateral conditions $=$ the $S$-matrix at
infinity)} Suppose $T_{n}<\infty$ for all $n$, and truncate to $M$
channels. The sub-Markov Feller extensions of the minimal process are
exactly the Feller--Wentzell lateral conditions at the $M$ regular
ends: at each $\infty_{n}$ a convex datum (absorption, killing,
reflection, stickiness, and a jump-in measure $\nu_{n}$ on
$\mathcal E$), the jump-in measures allowed to charge \emph{other}
channels. The conservative extensions are those with no
absorption/killing; a packet escaping in channel $n$ then re-enters
distributed according to $\nu_{n}$---a channel-\emph{mixing} return
law, the stochastic mirror of the unitary $S$-matrix at infinity of
Theorem~\ref{thm:channels}, and different lateral data reproduce the
regulator-dependence of the returned mixture seen in
Fig.~\ref{fig:channels}. \emph{[Finite $M$: proved, via Feller's
lateral conditions and Freidlin--Wentzell gluing; countable $M$:
proved, sketch, via It\^o excursion theory with a summability condition
on the excursion entrance laws; the completeness claim---that all
Feller extensions arise this way---is standard for finite $M$ and
conjectural for countable $M$.]}
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i)--(iii) are channelwise reductions. The direct sum has no
interaction between channels, so every path quantity is computed in the
channel where the process starts; Lemma~\ref{lem:feller} applied with
potential $V_{1}+E_{n}$ (spectral shift $E\mapsto E-E_{n}$) gives (i)
and (ii) verbatim, and Lemma~\ref{lem:app-classeq} gives the class
equality. Uniqueness in (ii): at a natural end no lateral condition
exists (Feller \cite{Feller1952,Feller1957}), channel by channel, and a
Feller extension whose generator agrees with the direct sum on
$C_{c}(\bigsqcup_{n}I_{n}^{\circ})$ respects the direct-sum structure away
from the boundary (no interior channel-coupling jumps); since no boundary is
accessible, the minimal realization is the only one. For (iii), the
deficiency equation in channel $n$ is
$(h_{1}-(\pm\ii-E_{n}))u=0$; to leading WKB order the modulus is
$|u_{n}|^{2}\approx[2(E-E_{n}-V_{1})]^{-1/2}=\tfrac12 m_{n}$, and the
scale mass is $\int^{\infty}\dd\tau_{n}=T_{n}$.

(iv), finite $M$. Each $\infty_{n}$ is regular by (i). Feller's
classification of lateral conditions at a regular boundary of a
generalized second-order operator \cite{Feller1957} provides, for one
interval, the general boundary datum including a jump-in measure; the
multi-interval (graph) case, with boundary points allowed to
communicate, is Freidlin--Wentzell gluing (Freidlin and Wentzell,
Ann.\ Probab.\ \textbf{21} (1993) 2215), which parametrizes all Feller
processes agreeing with the minimal one in the interior by Wentzell
data at the vertices; jump-in to interior points is supplied by the
It\^o excursion construction of instantaneous-return processes (It\^o,
Proc.\ Sixth Berkeley Symp.\ III (1972) 225; Blumenthal,
\emph{Excursions of Markov Processes}, Birkh\"auser 1992). Countable
$M$: the same construction applies end by end; one must check that
concatenation over countably many ends accumulates no infinitely many
boundary visits in finite time, for which a uniform lower bound on mean
return times (automatic when $\inf_{n}T_{n}>0$, in general a
summability condition on the entrance laws) suffices. We assert
completeness only for finite $M$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the mirror is structural, not a bijection]
The quantum completions form the unitary group
$U(\mathcal K_{+},\mathcal K_{-})$, $\dim\mathcal K_{\pm}=\infty$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:channels}); the conservative stochastic completions
form the convex set of Markov gluing data $(\nu_{n})$. As already in
one dimension ($U(1)$ versus Feller's convex family,
Lemma~\ref{lem:feller}(iii)), these are \emph{parallel
infinite-dimensional families attached to the same boundary objects},
in canonical correspondence at the level of which channels communicate
through infinity, not isomorphic as spaces.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[conservative / transient / explosive trichotomy]
The hierarchy of Sec.~\ref{sec:interacting} is reproduced by the clock
picture: in channel $n$ the drifted model is Brownian motion with unit
drift on $[0,T_{n})$; $T_{n}=\infty$ with $\beta=1$ is
\emph{conservative and transient} (the quadratic-ghost cascade,
Theorem~\ref{thm:quadratic}), $T_{n}<\infty$ is \emph{explosive}, and a
confining channel (no escape interval) is \emph{recurrent}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Central potentials: the sphere at infinity, resolved into
channels}
\label{app:multiD-central}

\begin{corollary}[central case; proved]\label{cor:app-central}
Let $H=-\tfrac12\Delta-c|x|^{\alpha}$ on $\mathbb R^{d}$, $d\ge2$,
$c>0$, $\alpha>2$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:central}). In the angular
decomposition the radial channel $\ell$ carries the escape diffusion
with $v_{\ell}(r)^{2}=2(E+cr^{\alpha}-L^{2}/(2r^{2}))$,
$L^{2}=(\ell+\tfrac{d-2}{2})^{2}-\tfrac14$, on $r\ge r_{\ell}$ beyond
the outer turning point. Then every channel end is regular, with
\[
T_{\ell}\;\asymp\;C_{\alpha}\,c^{-\frac{2}{\alpha+2}}\,
L^{\frac{2-\alpha}{2+\alpha}}\;\xrightarrow[\ell\to\infty]{}\;0,
\]
so $T^{*}=\sup_{\ell}T_{\ell}<\infty$: the classification is uniform
across the entire sphere of directions. All conclusions of
Theorem~\ref{thm:app-separable} hold with $n\mapsto\ell$; in particular
the conservative completions of the central malicious ghost are
angular-momentum-mixing return laws at $r=\infty$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Substituting $r=\lambda\rho$ with $\lambda=(L^{2}/c)^{1/(\alpha+2)}$
equalizes the potential and centrifugal scales, giving
$T_{\ell}=c^{-1/2}\lambda^{1-\alpha/2}(C_{\alpha}+o(1))$ with
$C_{\alpha}=\int_{\rho_{0}}^{\infty}
(2(\rho^{\alpha}-\tfrac12\rho^{-2}))^{-1/2}\dd\rho<\infty$ (convergent
at infinity since $\alpha>2$, at the simple turning point as
$(\rho-\rho_0)^{-1/2}$). Numerical check ($\alpha=4$, $c=1$, $E=0$;
\texttt{scripts/appD\_escape\_dictionary.py}): $C_{\alpha}=0.964$ and
$T_{\ell}$ at $L^{2}=10^{2},10^{4},10^{6}$ is $0.447,0.208,0.096$,
successive ratios $2.154$ against the predicted $10^{1/3}=2.154$. The
rest is Theorem~\ref{thm:app-separable}.
\end{proof}

Thus for central potentials the anticipated per-direction $T(E,\omega)$
collapses to the channel family $T_{\ell}$: the boundary at infinity is
not the geometric sphere $S^{d-1}$ but its harmonic resolution, and the
uniformity $T^{*}<\infty$ is automatic---the multidimensional situation
in which the generalization is \emph{complete}.

\subsection{Bounded channel mixing: the stochastic Kato theorem}
\label{app:multiD-bounded}

Theorem~\ref{thm:kato} says a bounded mixing preserves the deficiency
indices. The stochastic counterpart replaces the frozen channel label
by a jump process with \emph{bounded intensity}. Fix measurable rates
$q(x;n\to m)\ge0$ with $\sup_{x,n}\sum_{m}q(x;n\to
m)\le\bar q<\infty$, supported on open channels; realize $(X_{t},n_{t})$
by thinning an intensity-$\bar q$ Poisson clock, the path solving the
frozen channel SDE between accepted switches and continuous across them.

\begin{theorem}[stability under bounded mixing; proved]
\label{thm:app-kato}
For the switched escape process: \textbf{(a)} if $T_{n}=\infty$ for one
(hence every) channel, the process is conservative for \emph{every}
admissible bounded-rate law; \textbf{(b)} if
$(\mathrm U):\ T^{*}:=\sup_{n}T_{n}<\infty$, then it explodes a.s.\ for
every admissible law, with $\EE[\zeta]\le h/p_{*}<\infty$ where
$h=T^{*}+1$ and $p_{*}=\ee^{-\bar q h}
\Prob(\varepsilon\sup_{s\le h}|W_{s}|\le\tfrac12)>0$ ($\beta=1$; the
analogous positive constant for $\beta=0$). Hence under (U) the
dictionary survives bounded mixing verbatim---the stochastic mirror of
Kato stability of the deficiency indices (Theorem~\ref{thm:kato},
\cite{KatoBook}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) The candidate switch times are Poisson($\bar q$), so a.s.\ finitely
many switches in $[0,t]$; if $\zeta\le t$ the explosion instant lies in
an inter-candidate interval on which the path is a frozen channel
diffusion with natural end (Lemma~\ref{lem:feller}, $T=\infty$),
inaccessible in finite time---a null event, and a countable union of
null events is null. (b) On windows $[kh,(k+1)h)$ let $A_{k}$ be the
event of no candidate in the window and
$\varepsilon\sup_{s\le h}|W_{kh+s}-W_{kh}|\le\tfrac12$;
$\Prob(A_{k}\mid\mathcal F_{kh})\ge p_{*}$. On $A_{k}$, in the clock
coordinate (proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:feller}) $Z_{s}\ge s-\tfrac12$, and
$s=T_{n}+\tfrac12\le T^{*}+\tfrac12<h$ forces $Z$ to reach $T_{n}$:
explosion inside the window. Thus
$\Prob(\zeta>(k+1)h\mid\zeta>kh)\le1-p_{*}$, giving the geometric
bound. The argument never needs which channels are visited---only (U)
and open-channel support.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[when (U) is automatic; proved]\label{lem:app-U}
If for $x\ge R$: $-V_{1}(x)\ge c_{1}x^{2+\epsilon}$ and
$-V_{1}'(x)\ge c_{3}x^{1+\epsilon}$ ($\epsilon>0$, $V_{1}\in C^{1}$
strictly decreasing), and $T(E)<\infty$, then
$\sup_{E'\le E}T(E')<\infty$; so (U) holds for every transverse
spectrum. This covers all power-law potentials
$-V_{1}\sim cx^{2+\epsilon}$, hence the paper's representative models.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
With $\mu=-E'$ and turning point $x'$ ($-V_{1}(x')=\mu$, so
$x'\le x_{g}:=(\mu/c_{1})^{1/(2+\epsilon)}$ and $x'\to\infty$ as
$\mu\to\infty$), integrate the lower bound $-V_{1}'\ge c_{3}y^{1+\epsilon}$
over $[x',x]$: $-\mu-V_{1}(x)\ge\tfrac{c_{3}}{2+\epsilon}
(x^{2+\epsilon}-x'^{2+\epsilon})$. Substituting $x=x'w$,
$T(E')=\int_{x'}^{\infty}\dd x/\sqrt{2(-\mu-V_{1})}\le
C\,x'^{-\epsilon/2}\int_{1}^{\infty}\dd w/\sqrt{w^{2+\epsilon}-1}
=C''x'^{-\epsilon/2}\to0$ (the $w$-integral converges); so $T(E')\to0$ as
$E'\to-\infty$, and $T$ is finite and continuous on compact $E'$-ranges
(simple turning points).
\end{proof}

\subsection{The obstruction: unbounded channel transfer}
\label{app:multiD-obstruction}

Everything so far generalizes. What follows is the exact point of
failure, proved by construction.

\begin{lemma}[channel-riding reduction; proved]\label{lem:app-riding}
Let $n(\cdot):[x_{*},\infty)\to\mathbb N$ be measurable, locally
constant off a discrete set, with $n(x)$ open at $x$. The
\emph{position-locked} switched process (switch to $n(x)$ upon reaching
$x$; a limit of bounded-rate laws with rates $\to\infty$, \emph{outside}
Theorem~\ref{thm:app-kato}) is the one-dimensional generalized
diffusion with effective speed
$v_{\rm eff}(x)=\sqrt{2(E-E_{n(x)}-V_{1}(x))}$, classified by
Lemma~\ref{lem:feller} with the \emph{effective time of flight}
$T_{\rm eff}=\int_{x_{*}}^{\infty}\dd x/v_{\rm eff}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Scale-and-speed constructions need only a strictly increasing
continuous scale and a Radon speed measure (\cite{ItoMcKean}, \S4);
$v_{\rm eff}$ is measurable, locally bounded and locally bounded away
from $0$ on the open region, and the regular diffusion it defines
coincides with the switched process between switch points by locality.
The classification integrals are as in Lemma~\ref{lem:feller}
($\Sigma=N=T_{\rm eff}^{2}$ for $\beta=0$).
\end{proof}

$T_{\rm eff}$ is a \emph{path functional of the coupled mechanism}, not
a function of $\{T_{n}\}$. The next two propositions make the
underdetermination rigorous in both directions.

\begin{proposition}[Counterexample I: all channels explosive, coupled
conservative; proved]\label{prop:app-ce1}
There exist $V_{1}$ (continuous, non-increasing to $-\infty$, and---after a
slight shelf tilt and corner mollification, which do not affect the
estimates---strictly decreasing and $C^{1}$, meeting the regularity of
Theorem~\ref{thm:dictionary}) and a
transverse spectrum $(E_{n})$ with $\sup_{n}T_{n}\le\tfrac32$ (every
frozen channel explosive, uniformly---(U) holds), yet a position-locked
riding law gives $T_{\rm eff}=\infty$: the coupled process is
\emph{conservative}. So ``$T_{n}<\infty\ \forall n\Rightarrow$
explosive'' is \emph{false} for unbounded-rate transfer, even under
(U); the bounded-rate hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:app-kato} cannot
be dropped.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take $E=0$, $E_{n}=16^{n}$, and the shelf potential
$V_{1}=-E_{n}-\tfrac12$ on $[2n,2n+1]$, linear on $[2n+1,2n+2]$
($n\ge1$). Regularity: $|V_{1}'|\asymp16^{n}$ on the $n$-th drop while
$|V_{1}|^{3/2}\asymp16^{3n/2}$, so $|V_{1}'||V_{1}|^{-3/2}\to0$. Channel
$m$ is open from its shelf onward, $x_{m}=2m$, and
$T_{m}\le\tfrac1{\sqrt1}+\tfrac{\sqrt{1+2(E_{m+1}-E_{m})}-1}{E_{m+1}-E_{m}}+\sum_{n>m}
(\tfrac1{\sqrt{2(E_{n}-E_{m})}}+\tfrac2{\sqrt{E_{n}}})\le\tfrac32$
(numerically $T_{1}=1.17$, $T_{3}=1.01$, $T_{8}=1.00001$;
\texttt{scripts/appD\_escape\_dictionary.py}). Riding
$n(x)=n$ on $[2n,2n+2)$: on shelf $n$ the effective speed is $1$ over
unit length, so $T_{\rm eff}\ge\sum_{n}1=\infty$;
Lemmas~\ref{lem:app-riding} and~\ref{lem:feller} finish.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Counterexample II: all channels conservative,
coupled explosive---ghost-specific; proved]\label{prop:app-ce2}
Let $V_{1}\equiv0$ on $[1,\infty)$, $E=1$, and $h_{\perp}$ a
\emph{ghost} oscillator, $E_{n}=-(n+\tfrac12)$, unbounded below. Every
frozen channel has $T_{n}=\infty$ (conservative). The riding law
$n(x)=\lceil x^{2+\epsilon}\rceil$ gives
$v_{\rm eff}(x)^{2}=2(E+n(x)+\tfrac12)\ge2x^{2+\epsilon}$, so
$T_{\rm eff}\le\int^{\infty}x^{-1-\epsilon/2}\dd x/\sqrt2<\infty$: the
coupled process is \emph{explosive}. The mechanism extracts unbounded
energy from the transverse ghost---the stochastic realization of the
channel-tail escape of Sec.~\ref{sec:multiD} (the $\lambda<0$ PU
climbing through arbitrarily high mode-2 channels).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from Lemmas~\ref{lem:app-riding} and~\ref{lem:feller}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[ghosts are necessary for Counterexample II; proved
under (D),(R)]\label{thm:app-ghost}
Suppose $h_{\perp}$ is bounded below, $E_{n}\ge E_{0}$, let
$\bar v(x)^{2}=2(E-E_{0}-V_{1}(x))$ dominate every channel speed, and
assume $(\mathrm D):\ \int^{\infty}\dd x/\bar v=\infty$ and
$\limsup_{x\to\infty}\bar v'(x)<\infty$ (which holds if
$-V_{1}'\le K\sqrt{1+|V_{1}|}$, i.e.\ $-V_{1}'\le K\bar v$),
and $(\mathrm R)$: every channel reflects at the common base $x_{*}$ (no
channel reflects above $x_{*}$). Then \emph{every} switched escape
process---arbitrary adapted switching with countably many switches,
including position-locked riding and, \emph{provided the switching is
itself non-explosive} (finitely many switches in every finite time interval
a.s.; for a birth-chain coupling, $\sum_{n}1/q_{n}=\infty$), any unbounded
rates---is conservative \emph{in the spatial coordinate} $X$ (no escape to
$x=\infty$); escape to the channel tail $n=\infty$ is governed separately by
Proposition~\ref{prop:app-tail}. In particular, at-most-quadratic escape ends with
non-ghost transverse dynamics are stable under
channel coupling: under $(\mathrm R)$ and non-explosive switching the
conservative leg of the dictionary
is robust, the explosive leg is not. (The Zeno rates $q_{n}=2^{n}$, for which
$\sum_{n}1/q_{n}<\infty$, show the non-explosive-switching hypothesis cannot be
dropped: the label reaches $n=\infty$ in finite time at bounded $x$.)
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Khasminskii test with the dominating clock (Khasminskii,
\emph{Stochastic Stability of Differential Equations}, 2nd ed.,
Springer 2012, Thm.~3.5). Let $\bar\tau(x)=\int_{x_{*}}^{x}\dd y/\bar v$
and $\varphi=\bar\tau$ for $x\ge x_{*}+1$, smoothed to constant near the
base so $\varphi'\ge0$ vanishes at every reflection point (all at $x_{*}$ by
$(\mathrm R)$);
$\varphi\uparrow\infty$ by (D). For any channel $n$ open at $x$
($v_{n}\le\bar v$), using $v_{n}v_{n}'=-V_{1}'=\bar v\,\bar v'$ (shared
across channels),
$v_{n}(v_{n}\varphi')'=\bar v'(1-v_{n}^{2}/\bar v^{2})\le C$, so
$L_{n}\varphi\le\beta+\tfrac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}C=:C'$ uniformly in
$n,x$. Since $X$ is continuous across switches and $\varphi'$ vanishes
at reflection points, $\varphi(X_{t\wedge\zeta_{k}})-C't$ is a
supermartingale up to the exit time $\zeta_{k}$ of $[x_{*},k]$;
$\EE\,\varphi(X_{t\wedge\zeta_{k}})\le\varphi(x_{0})+C't$ while
$\varphi(k)\to\infty$ gives $\Prob(\zeta_{k}\le t)\to0$: no explosion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[where separability is and is not needed]
\emph{Not needed} for the per-channel statements under bounded mixing
(Theorem~\ref{thm:app-kato}), nor for the conservative side when
$h_{\perp}$ is bounded below and the end at most quadratic
(Theorem~\ref{thm:app-ghost}, under $(\mathrm R)$ and non-explosive switching, no coupling hypothesis).
\emph{Needed}:
some control of transfer (bounded rate, or transverse boundedness
below) for the explosive side; Proposition~\ref{prop:app-ce1} shows
$\{T_{n}\}$ does not determine the coupled classification under
unbounded transfer, and Proposition~\ref{prop:app-ce2} shows the
conservative side fails only by extracting unbounded transverse energy,
i.e.\ only for ghosts (Theorem~\ref{thm:app-ghost}). The deciding
object is the time-of-flight functional $T_{\rm eff}$ along the actual
coupled mechanism---one integral per escape \emph{path family}, not per
channel.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The second boundary: the channel tail}
\label{app:multiD-tail}

In $d>1$ the state space $\{(x,n)\}$ has, besides the channel ends at
$x=\infty$, a second end: $n\to\infty$ at co-moving $x$.

\begin{proposition}[tail end of the channel chain; proved]
\label{prop:app-tail}
Model the transverse transfer as a minimal birth chain on $\mathbb N$
with upward rates $q_{n}>0$. The tail $n=\infty$ is reached in finite
time a.s.\ iff $\sum_{n}1/q_{n}<\infty$, and the mean climbing time is
$\sum_{n}1/q_{n}$ (Feller's explosion criterion; \cite{KarlinTaylor}, Ch.~14).
The tail carries its own time of flight
$T_{\rm tail}=\sum_{n}1/q_{n}$, in exact formal analogy with
$T=\int\dd x/v$---\emph{the dictionary acquires a second leg of the
same shape}, but its datum $q_{n}$ comes from the coupling, not from
any integral of the potential.
\end{proposition}

\begin{openproblem}[coupled dictionary; conjecture]
\label{conj:app-coupled}
We conjecture: for the switched escape process with adapted rates
derived from a (possibly unbounded) coupling, the process explodes iff
the combined time-of-flight functional is finite along some accessible
escape route, over routes carrying both spatial ends (classified by
$T_{\rm eff}$, Lemma~\ref{lem:app-riding}) and the tail end (classified
by $T_{\rm tail}$-type sums, Proposition~\ref{prop:app-tail}). The
results above are the proved instances; the general statement is open
because accessibility of a route for the given rates is a
large-deviations question we have not settled.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{remark}[what this says about Open Problem~\ref{op:pu}]
The paper's remaining gap---whether the finite-channel indices survive
$M\to\infty$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:stability},
Corollary~\ref{cor:dichotomy})---is the quantum image of the dichotomy
proved here. For $\lambda>0$ PU the classical escape locks onto a
definite kinetic direction (bounded channel occupation), the regime of
Theorem~\ref{thm:app-kato}(b)/(U) where explosivity is stable---so the
analysis \emph{supports} deficiency $(\infty,\infty)$ but does not
prove it (the couplings are polynomial, not bounded; the needed
strengthening is the open tail control). For $\lambda<0$ PU the escape
is channel-tail riding (Proposition~\ref{prop:app-ce2}/
Theorem~\ref{thm:app-ghost}), where the classical and quantum legs can
and apparently do split (classical blowup, quantum essential
self-adjointness at every finite $M$): zero-point transverse energy
renormalizes the rates $q_{n}$, closing the tail. The framework locates
the split at the tail end and explains its mechanism, but does not
decide the full-operator question (Open Problem~\ref{op:pu}). The
direction of robustness proved here (conservative side stable,
explosive side fragile) matches the paper's numerics: both completion
candidates sit on the classically-explosive-but-quantum-safe side,
never the reverse.
\end{remark}

\section{The extension--lateral parametrization}
\label{app:completions}

This appendix proves Theorem~\ref{thm:extlat} and supplies the full
parametrization behind Sec.~\ref{sec:completions}: the boundary unitary
of a self-adjoint extension (Proposition~\ref{prop:bdyS}), the flux
functor onto the slice $\mathcal C$ of the Feller--Wentzell cone, the
four exclusions each tied to its exact invariant---including the
maximal-dissipative matrix-ball picture---and the honest caveats
($E$-covariance and the $\varepsilon>0$ jump-rate normalization).
Status conventions as in Appendix~\ref{app:multiD}: statements are
\emph{[proved]} unless tagged \emph{[proved, sketch]} or
\emph{[conjecture]}; everything at the class level is \emph{[proved]},
and the only WKB input the class-level algebra uses is the
Olver-normalized in/out frame of \S\ref{app:completions-setup} (which
fixes the $\mathrm{SU}(1,1)$ frame freedom so that the moduli
$|S_{ij}|^{2}$ are well-defined); the finer dynamical asymptotics enter
only in Proposition~\ref{prop:revival}.

\subsection{Boundary data on the two sides}
\label{app:completions-setup}

Throughout, $\tau\psi=-\tfrac12\psi''+V\psi$ on $I=(r_{-},r_{+})$ with
$r_{+}=+\infty$ an escaping end of the mechanism model of
Theorem~\ref{thm:dictionary} in its limit-circle regime ($V\in C^{2}$, $V\to-\infty$ eventually
monotonically, $|V'|\,|V|^{-3/2}$ bounded and $|V''|\,|V|^{-3/2}$
integrable---the added $C^{2}$ control of leg~(2)) and classical time of flight
$T_{+}(E)=\int^{\infty}\dd x/\sqrt{2(E-V)}<\infty$, so that $r_{+}$ is
limit circle for $\tau$ \cite{ReedSimon2} and a \emph{regular} Feller
boundary for the escape diffusion (Lemma~\ref{lem:feller}(i)). Two
cases: $n=1$ ($r_{-}$ confining, hence limit point with no boundary
data; deficiency $(1,1)$, extensions $\cong U(1)$) and $n=2$
($r_{-}=-\infty$ also escaping with $T_{-}(E)<\infty$, e.g.\
$V=-x^{4}$; deficiency $(2,2)$, extensions $\cong U(2)$). Fix once and
for all a real $E$ with $V<E$ on a neighborhood of each escaping
end---the same $E$ at which the escape diffusion
$\dd X=\sqrt{2(E-V(X))}\circ(\dd t+\varepsilon\,\dd W)$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:feller} (or its driftless variant, scale $s=\tau_{\rm
clock}$, speed $m=2\,\dd x/v$) is built. For $n=1$ the confining end
plays the role of the reflecting base point $x_{0}$; for $n=2$ both
ends carry boundary data.

\paragraph{Quantum boundary data \emph{[proved]}.}
For $\psi,\varphi\in D(\tau_{\max})$ the Lagrange bracket
$[\bar\psi,\varphi]_{x}=\bar\psi\varphi'-\bar\psi'\varphi$ has limits
at each end, and Green's identity localizes the boundary form there:
\begin{equation}
\langle\tau\psi,\varphi\rangle-\langle\psi,\tau\varphi\rangle
=\tfrac12\Bigl([\bar\psi,\varphi]_{r_{+}}
-[\bar\psi,\varphi]_{r_{-}}\Bigr)
=:\,\ii\,\Omega(\psi,\varphi).
\label{eq:greenform}
\end{equation}
$\Omega$ here (the boundary current form, distinct from the vacuum
$\Omega$ of Sec.~\ref{sec:pu} and the squared-frequency sum $\Omega=\omega_1^{2}+\omega_2^{2}$
of Sec.~\ref{sec:multiD}) descends to the $2n$-complex-dimensional boundary space
$\mathcal B=D(\tau_{\max})/D(\tau_{\min})$ (with $\tau$ the
Sturm--Liouville expression, not the rescaled clock $\tau=\kappa t$ of
Sec.~\ref{sec:ep} or the Feller clock coordinate of Lemma~\ref{lem:feller}), where it is a nondegenerate
Hermitian form of signature $(n,n)$; self-adjoint extensions are in
bijection with the \emph{maximal $\Omega$-neutral} subspaces of
$\mathcal B$ (complex-symplectic/boundary-triple theory
\cite{EverittMarkus,BHS,Zettl}). Physically, $\Omega(\psi,\psi)$ is the
total \emph{outward probability current} at the ends: with
$j(\psi)=\operatorname{Im}(\bar\psi\psi')$ one has
$\partial_{t}|\psi_{t}|^{2}+\partial_{x}j(\psi_{t})=0$ pointwise in $I$
for \emph{every} extension (all extensions act as the same differential
expression), and $\Omega(\psi,\psi)=j(\psi)(r_{+})-j(\psi)(r_{-})$.

At each escaping end, the solution germs
$N_{E}^{\pm}=\{u:\tau u=Eu\text{ near }r_{\pm}\}$ form a
$2$-complex-dimensional space on which the per-end current form
\[
J^{r_{+}}(u,w)=\tfrac{1}{2\ii}\,[\bar u,w]_{r_{+}},\qquad
J^{r_{-}}(u,w)=-\tfrac{1}{2\ii}\,[\bar u,w]_{r_{-}}
\quad(\text{sign: current \emph{toward} the end})
\]
is constant in $x$, Hermitian, of signature $(1,1)$. A real pair
$u_{1},u_{2}$ with Wronskian $1$ gives combinations
$\psi_{\pm}=u_{1}\pm\ii u_{2}$ with $J(\psi_{\pm},\psi_{\pm})=\pm1$,
$J(\psi_{+},\psi_{-})=0$; but the $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb R)$ freedom in that
pair induces an end-local $\mathrm{SU}(1,1)$ freedom in
$(\psi_{+},\psi_{-})$, under which the scattering moduli $|S_{ij}|^{2}$
below are \emph{not} invariant, so an arbitrary real pair does not by
itself fix $R=|S_{ii}|^{2}$. We therefore \emph{pin} the frame to the
physical in/out waves: under the mechanism hypotheses (with $V\in C^{2}$
and $|V''|\,|V|^{-3/2}$ integrable, as in Prop.~\ref{prop:revival}) the Liouville--Green
solutions $\psi_{\pm}\sim v^{-1/2}\ee^{\pm\ii\int v\,\dd x}$,
$v=\sqrt{2(E-V)}$, exist with Olver's error bounds \cite{Olver} and are
canonical up to a common $U(1)$ phase (their real and imaginary parts form
a Wronskian-$1$ real pair), and we take $\psi_{\pm}$ to be these. Under the
residual diagonal $U(1)$ the moduli $|S_{ij}|^{2}$ \emph{are} invariant, so
$R=|S_{ii}|^{2}$ and the flux functor below are well-defined; this
Olver-normalized frame is the only WKB input the algebra requires, and the
class-level statements of Theorem~\ref{thm:extlat} (unitarity of $S$, double
stochasticity, the $n=1$ total reflection) are frame-independent regardless.
Write $u=a\,\psi_{+}+b\,\psi_{-}$ near each escaping end: $a$
is the current-normalized \emph{incident} (toward-the-boundary)
amplitude, $b$ the \emph{returned} one, and $J(u,u)=|a|^{2}-|b|^{2}$.
Because $T_{\pm}<\infty$ (limit circle), the trace map
$\btr:N_{E}:=\bigoplus_{i}N_{E}^{(i)}\to\mathcal B$ (germs to boundary
data) is an isomorphism for \emph{every} $E\in\mathbb{R}$ \cite{Zettl}.

\paragraph{Stochastic boundary data \emph{[proved]}; Feller--Wentzell.}
At a regular boundary of the escape diffusion, with generator
$G=D_{m}D_{s}$ as in Appendix~\ref{app:feller}, the sub-Markov $C_{0}$
extensions of the minimal (absorbed) semigroup are in bijection with
\emph{lateral conditions}
\begin{equation}
p_{0}\,u(r_{+})\;-\;p_{1}\,\frac{\dd u}{\dd s}(r_{+})
\;+\;p_{2}\,(Gu)(r_{+})
\;=\;\int\bigl(u(y)-u(r_{+})\bigr)\,\nu(\dd y),
\label{eq:lateral}
\end{equation}
$p_{0},p_{1},p_{2}\ge0$, $\nu\ge0$ a measure on
$\bar I\setminus\{r_{+}\}$ (the interior and, in the two-ended problem,
the other boundary point), not all data zero, modulo positive scaling
\cite{Feller1952,Feller1957,ItoMcKean}. Terminology: $p_{0}$
\emph{killing} (absorbing $=p_{0}$ alone, i.e.\ $u(r_{+})=0$, the
minimal explosive semigroup; elastic $=p_{0},p_{1}>0$), $p_{1}$
\emph{reflection} (the scale derivative $\dd u/\dd s$ in
\eqref{eq:lateral} is taken into the interval---inward-pointing at the
right end $r_{+}$---so the datum carries the standard endpoint-symmetric
Feller--Wentzell signs; for $p_{2}=\nu=0$ the elastic condition is then
$\dd u/\dd s=(p_{0}/p_{1})u\ge0$ inward, the sub-Markov sign), $p_{2}$ \emph{stickiness} (the boundary carries a
speed-measure atom $p_{2}\delta_{r_{+}}$: the process spends positive
Lebesgue time at $r_{+}$), $\nu$ \emph{jump-in} (re-entry, possibly at
the other boundary: Feller's ``circle-type'' passage). Applying
\eqref{eq:lateral} to $u=\mathbf 1$ shows: \emph{the extension is
conservative iff $p_{0}=0$ at every regular end} (no killing anywhere;
$\nu$ may be arbitrary). Denote by $\mathcal W$ the set of lateral data
(one datum per regular end), a cone of infinite dimension (because of
$\nu$).

\subsection{The boundary unitary of a completion}
\label{app:completions-unitary}

\begin{proposition}[boundary scattering matrix at fixed energy; proved]
\label{prop:bdyS}
Fix $E$ as above and let $\tilde H$ be any self-adjoint extension, with
maximal $\Omega$-neutral boundary subspace $L\subset\mathcal B$. Let
$\mathcal S_{L}=\{u\in N_{E}:\btr u\in L\}$ (germ solutions at energy
$E$ admissible for $\tilde H$), and let $a=(a_{i})$,
$b=(b_{i})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}$ be the incident/returned amplitude
coordinates at the escaping ends. Then:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] $\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathcal S_{L}=n$, and for
$u\in\mathcal S_{L}$,
$\sum_{i}\bigl(|a_{i}|^{2}-|b_{i}|^{2}\bigr)=0$ \emph{(exact current
conservation)};
\item[(ii)] the map $u\mapsto a$ is injective on $\mathcal S_{L}$,
hence bijective onto $\mathbb{C}^{n}$, and there is a unique
$S_{\tilde H}(E)\in U(n)$ with $b=S_{\tilde H}(E)\,a$ on
$\mathcal S_{L}$;
\item[(iii)] $\tilde H\mapsto S_{\tilde H}(E)$ is a bijection from the
self-adjoint extensions onto $U(n)$. Separated boundary conditions
correspond to diagonal $S$ (the torus $U(1)^{n}\subset U(n)$); for
$n=1$, $S_{\vartheta}(E)=\ee^{\ii\alpha(\vartheta,E)}$ recovers the $U(1)$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:dictionary}(2)(a).
\end{itemize}
For comparison, the same construction with ``maximal neutral'' relaxed
to ``maximal nonnegative'' ($\Omega(u,u)\ge0$ on $L$, i.e.\ net outward
current $\ge0$: maximal-dissipative extensions, contraction semigroups
$\ee^{-\ii t\tilde H}$) yields exactly the closed matrix ball
$\norm{S}\le1$ \cite{BHS}; the self-adjoint family is its unitary
boundary.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) The trace map $N_{E}\to\mathcal B$ is an isomorphism (limit circle
at each escaping end; the confining end contributes no data), so
$\mathcal S_{L}\cong L$, of dimension $n$. For $u\in\mathcal S_{L}$:
$\Omega(u,u)=0$ by neutrality of $L$, and
$\Omega(u,u)=\sum_{i}J^{(i)}(u,u)=\sum_{i}(|a_{i}|^{2}-|b_{i}|^{2})$ by
constancy of the per-end bracket on solutions.

(ii) If $a=0$ then (i) gives $\sum|b_{i}|^{2}=0$, so $u=0$: injective;
by dimension count, onto $\mathbb{C}^{n}$. Define $S$ by $b=Sa$; (i)
says $\norm{Sa}=\norm{a}$ for all $a$, i.e.\ $S\in U(n)$.

(iii) $\mathcal S_{L}$ is recovered from $S$ as
$\{a\,\psi_{+}+Sa\,\psi_{-}\}$ and $L=\btr\mathcal S_{L}$; conversely,
any maximal neutral subspace of a signature-$(n,n)$ space is the graph
of a unitary from the positive to the negative part (if $(0,b')\in L$,
neutrality forces $b'=0$; then the $a$-projection is bijective and the
graph map is isometric). In the $(a,b)$ coordinates at energy $E$,
$\Omega=\mathrm{diag}(+I_{n},-I_{n})$, so maximal neutral subspaces
$\leftrightarrow U(n)$, and by the trace isomorphism these are exactly
the extension subspaces. Separated conditions constrain each end's data
independently, giving block-diagonal (here $1\times1$, i.e.\ diagonal)
$S$. For the dissipative case replace ``isometric graph'' by
``contractive graph.''
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[$E$-covariance]\label{rem:Ecovariance}
The extension $\tilde H$ is fixed; the frames $\psi_{\pm}(E)$ rotate
with $E$ by an end-local $U(1,1)$ M\"obius action, under which
unitarity of $S(E)$ is preserved but the moduli $|S_{ij}(E)|$ in
general drift with $E$. Two subfamilies are $E$-uniform: separated
conditions (diagonal $S$ for all $E$) and the smooth gluing (the
periodic identification of Fig.~\ref{fig:explosion}: the glued
clock-circle problem has no defect at the seam, so $S$ is
antidiagonal---perfect transmission---at every $E$). The dictionary
below is stated at the mechanism energy $E$, the same $E$ at which the
escape diffusion itself is defined; this matching of reference energies
is intrinsic to the construction, not a defect.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The flux functor and the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:extlat}}
\label{app:completions-proof}

\begin{definition}[flux functor]\label{def:fluxfunctor}
For a self-adjoint extension $\tilde H$ define the lateral datum
$\Phi_{E}(\tilde H)\in\mathcal W$ by: at each escaping end $i$,
\[
p_{0}^{(i)}=0,\qquad p_{2}^{(i)}=0,\qquad
p_{1}^{(i)}=|S_{ii}(E)|^{2},\qquad
\nu^{(i)}=\sum_{j\ne i}|S_{ij}(E)|^{2}\,\delta_{r_{j}},
\]
i.e.\ conservative, instantaneous, boundary-supported, with reflection
weight $|S_{ii}|^{2}$ and passage-to-the-other-end weight
$|S_{ij}|^{2}$. (For $\varepsilon>0$ the pair $(p_{1},\nu)$ is to be
read projectively---re-entry at local-time rate $\nu/p_{1}$; in the
transport limit $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ the weights are literal
per-arrival branching probabilities. See Remark~\ref{rem:ratenorm}.)
\end{definition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:extlat}]
\emph{(1)--(3).} By Proposition~\ref{prop:bdyS},
$\tilde H\mapsto S(E)$ is a bijection onto $U(n)$, and $\Phi_{E}$
factors through it. For $n=1$, $|S|=1$ forces reflection weight $1$:
the constant map, with fiber the whole $U(1)$. (Equivalently, without
Proposition~\ref{prop:bdyS}: any separated real boundary condition
$\cos\vartheta\,\operatorname{tr}_{\partial,1}u+\sin\vartheta\,\operatorname{tr}_{\partial,2}u=0$ is real, so its
admissible germ line is spanned by a \emph{real} solution
$u=a\psi_{+}+\bar a\psi_{-}$, whence $|b/a|=1$: total reflection for
every $\vartheta$.) For $n=2$, double stochasticity of $(|S_{ij}|^{2})$
(row and column sums $1$, because $S$ is unitary) gives
$|S_{11}|^{2}=|S_{22}|^{2}=:R$ and $|S_{12}|^{2}=|S_{21}|^{2}=1-R$, so
the image lies in the reciprocal family of part~(3), which is contained
in $\mathcal C$ by construction of $\Phi_{E}$. Ontoness: the matrix
$S=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}\sqrt R&\ii\sqrt{1-R}\\
\ii\sqrt{1-R}&\sqrt R\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ is unitary for every
$R\in[0,1]$; $R=1$ is realized by the separated (e.g.\ Dirichlet)
completions and $R=0$ by the smooth gluing, whose stochastic shadow is
Feller's circle-type jump datum---the darning of the two boundary
points \cite{ChenFukushima}. Fibers: fixing the moduli of a $2\times2$
unitary leaves its four phases free up to one orthogonality relation
(columns orthogonal), a torus of dimension $3$.

\emph{(4): the four exclusions, each by its exact invariant.}

\emph{(c1) Killing ($p_{0}>0$: absorbing, elastic) is excluded by
unitarity.} If $p_{0}>0$, start the process at any interior $x$; since
the boundary is regular and accessible ($\Sigma<\infty$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:feller}(i)--(ii)), $\Prob_{x}(\text{hit the end}\le
t)>0$ for every $t>0$, and each boundary visit kills a positive
fraction of local-time mass, so $\EE_{x}[\mathbf 1_{\{\rm
alive\}}]<1$ for all $t>0$; under any correspondence matching total
probability this would equal
$\norm{\ee^{-\ii t\tilde H}\psi}^{2}/\norm{\psi}^{2}$, which a self-adjoint
(norm-preserving) evolution holds at $1$---so no self-adjoint extension
matches a killing datum. The dissipative
match is Proposition~\ref{prop:bdyS}: maximal nonnegative boundary
subspaces are contractive graphs $\norm{S}\le1$; $S=0$ is the condition
$b=0$ (no returned amplitude: every admissible germ purely outgoing),
the quantum radiation condition, whose semigroup loses exactly the
arriving flux, as does the absorbing/minimal explosive semigroup of
Theorem~\ref{thm:dictionary}(2)(c); $0<\norm{S}<1$ returns the fraction
$\norm{Sa}^{2}/\norm{a}^{2}<1$, as does the elastic condition
(per-arrival kill fraction $1-|S|^{2}$ in the transport limit). Hence
sub-unitary $\leftrightarrow$ sub-Markov, and the conservative slice is
matched by exactly the extreme points (the unitaries) of the ball.

\emph{(c2) Stickiness ($p_{2}>0$) is excluded by the measure class of
the state space.} For a sticky datum,
$\EE\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf 1_{\{X_{s}=r_{+}\}}\dd s>0$ after the first
boundary hit (speed atom $p_{2}\delta_{r_{+}}$; \cite{ItoMcKean},
\S3.7), so $\Prob(X_{s}=r_{+})>0$ on a time set of positive measure,
and the semigroup is symmetric only in $L^{2}(m+p_{2}\delta_{r_{+}})$;
every self-adjoint extension instead acts in $L^{2}(I,\dd x)$, whose
measure has no boundary atom: $|\psi_{t}|^{2}\dd x(\{r_{+}\})=0$ for
all $t$. A quantum counterpart would require enlarging the Hilbert
space to $L^{2}(I)\oplus\mathbb{C}$ (a boundary state)---extensions of
a \emph{different} symmetric operator, outside the $U(n)$ family
(Remark~\ref{rem:phasereceptacle}).

\emph{(c3) Interior jump-in ($\nu(I^{\circ})>0$) is excluded at the
classification level.} By \eqref{eq:greenform} the boundary form of
$\tau$ vanishes on compactly supported interior functions: the
self-adjointness constraint is a condition on \emph{end} data alone,
and the classification of extensions by maximal neutral subspaces of
the end boundary space is exhaustive, so no extension has a parameter
available to encode an interior re-entry point; $\Phi_{E}$'s jump
measures are supported on the ends by construction, and no refinement
through boundary data can do otherwise. (Semiclassically the same:
wave packets return through the ends.)

\emph{(c4) Asymmetric passage is excluded by unitarity again.} For
$U\in U(2)$ the matrix $(|U_{ij}|^{2})$ is doubly stochastic, hence
$|S_{12}|^{2}=|S_{21}|^{2}$ and $|S_{11}|^{2}=|S_{22}|^{2}$.
Independent lateral data at the two ends form a two-parameter family
$(R_{+},R_{-})$; the image is exactly its diagonal.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Dynamical content \emph{[proved, sketch]}}
\label{app:completions-dynamics}

\begin{proposition}[arrival and revival; proved, sketch]
\label{prop:revival}
For a WKB wave packet of mean energy $E$ launched toward end $i$: it
reaches the boundary after the classical time of flight ($\approx
T_{i}(E)$, the $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ explosion time of the escape
diffusion, Lemma~\ref{lem:feller}(ii)), and the fraction
$|S_{ji}(E)|^{2}$ of its probability re-emerges from end $j$ after the
finite round trip. This matches the $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ escape
diffusion with branching lateral datum $\Phi_{E}(\tilde H)$, for which
the boundary behavior per arrival is literally ``reflect w.p.~$R$ /
pass w.p.~$1-R$.'' The revival numerics of Fig.~\ref{fig:explosion}
(right)---Dirichlet wall returning from the right, periodic gluing from
the left---are the two extreme points $R=1,0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch (the only non-exact leg)]
Under the mechanism hypotheses, augmented by $V\in C^{2}$ with
$|V''|\,|V|^{-3/2}$ integrable (which implies, but is not implied by,
$V'(-V)^{-3/2}$ of bounded variation), the Liouville--Green solutions carry Olver-type error bounds
($\int|v^{-1/2}(v^{-1/2})''|\,\dd x<\infty$, whose integrand
$\tfrac{5}{16}q^{-5/2}(q')^{2}-\tfrac14 q^{-3/2}q''$ with $q=2(E-V)$ has
its $V''$ piece controlled by the $|V''|\,|V|^{-3/2}$ integrability and
its $(V')^{2}$ piece by the same, through
$\int(W')^{2}W^{-5/2}=-\tfrac23\bigl[W'W^{-3/2}\bigr]+\tfrac23\int W''W^{-3/2}$
($W=E-V$), the boundary term $|V'|\,|V|^{-3/2}$ bounded with
$V\to-\infty$ monotonically), so
$\psi_{\pm}=v^{-1/2}\ee^{\pm\ii\int v}(1+o(1))$ with controlled
remainders \cite{Olver}. A packet
$\int\chi(E')\,u_{E'}\,\ee^{-\ii E't}\,\dd E'$ built on admissible
solutions ($b(E')=S(E')a(E')$) has, by stationary phase, an incident
front arriving at the end at the classical time and returned fronts of
weight $|S_{ji}|^{2}$ emerging at end $j$, with corrections
$O(\text{window}^{-1})+o(1)_{\rm WKB}$; the discrete spectrum (spacing
$\pi/T_{\rm cross}$, Theorem~\ref{thm:dictionary}(2)(b)) does not
obstruct because the packet width covers many levels. On the stochastic
side, $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ reduces the escape diffusion to the
transport flow $\dot x=v$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:feller}(ii), drifted
variant: $\zeta\to T$
a.s.), for which the branching lateral datum acts per arrival with
probabilities $R,1-R$. The two evolutions then agree at the level of
arrival times and returned/passed mass fractions. This leg is
asymptotic by nature; the exact statements are
Theorem~\ref{thm:extlat}(1)--(4).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Caveats and the collapse mechanism}
\label{app:completions-caveats}

\begin{remark}[rate normalization at $\varepsilon>0$]\label{rem:ratenorm}
At $\varepsilon>0$ a regular boundary is visited on a local-time set,
so ``reflect w.p.~$R$ per bounce'' is ill-posed; the correct
$\nu$-versus-$p_{1}$ normalization is an excursion-theoretic convention
(jump at local-time rate $\propto(1-R)/R$), and only the
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$ limit makes the weights literal per-arrival
probabilities. We therefore claim the quantitative identification of
$R$ only in that limit; the class-level statements (which slice of the
cone, which ends linked, reciprocity) are exact for every
$\varepsilon>0$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[why the phases must collapse]\label{rem:phasecollapse}
The collapse of the $U(1)$ fibers is structural, not an artifact of the
map chosen. On the escaping end $v\ge v_{\min}>0$, so the quantum phase
$\int^{\infty}v\,\dd x=\infty$ diverges while the clock
$\int^{\infty}\dd x/v=T<\infty$ converges: the quantum boundary basis
$\psi_{\pm}$ winds \emph{infinitely often} approaching the end
(solutions have infinitely many zeros accumulating at $+\infty$),
whereas the diffusion's boundary basis---the $G$-harmonic functions $1$
and $s$---is monotone with finite boundary limits. No linear
identification of the two two-dimensional boundary spaces can be
canonical (there is no preferred ``zeroth winding''), and---once the frame
is pinned to the Olver in/out waves of \S\ref{app:completions-setup}, which
fixes the $\mathrm{SU}(1,1)$ freedom up to the residual diagonal
$U(1)$---the only quantities invariant under that residual and transferable
to the probabilistic side are the current moduli $|S_{ij}|^{2}$. The phase
is precisely the datum the $|\cdot|^{2}$ functor forgets.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[what would see the phases; conjecture]
\label{rem:phasereceptacle}
Two natural extensions of the stochastic side, both outside Feller's
lateral-condition cone for the escape diffusion in $L^{2}(m)$, are the
plausible receptacles for the lost data, and we record them as
\emph{conjectural} dictionary entries: (a) sticky data $p_{2}>0$
correspond to self-adjoint operators on $L^{2}(I)\oplus\mathbb{C}$ (a
boundary degree of freedom; Wentzell-type quantum boundary conditions),
not to any extension of $H$ in $L^{2}(I)$; (b) the Wigner delay
$\partial\alpha/\partial E$ of a fixed extension is the natural phase
shadow of boundary delay, but it can be negative and is not
constant-per-visit, so it does \emph{not} match any sub-Markov sticky
parameter---consistent with (c2) being an exact exclusion rather than a
near miss.
\end{remark}
